# Accountability - Feature Map

A one-page "what's in here" for the whole app. The app lives in a single file:
**`app.html`** (HTML + CSS + vanilla JS, ~5,000 lines; `index.html` is the landing
page). One config-gated dependency: the Supabase SDK, lazy-loaded ONLY if the
opt-in encrypted sync is configured and used - otherwise zero external requests
(fonts are self-hosted). Data is stored in the browser under the localStorage key
**`unfiltered_budget_v2`**. Nothing leaves the device unless sync is turned on,
and sync sends only ciphertext.

---

## Plan vs Track

Both tabs can end up creating transactions, which is what makes the boundary
confusing. The rule is **tense**, not mechanism:

- **Plan** is money that has not happened yet. Assigning, the four walls,
  Recurring, the membership sweep. Deciding what repeats is a plan, even though
  it eventually posts entries.
- **Track** is money that already happened. Logging, CSV import, the time ledger.
  Anything Recurring posts lands here like any other entry.

Each view states this at the top (`.tab-intro`) and points at the other, because
neither is guessable from the tab name alone.

`refreshCatSelects()` keeps every category picker in sync from one place. Ten
sites create categories and only some of them refreshed the Recurring picker, so
it could sit there saying "Add a category first" with four funded walls directly
above it. It preserves the current selection across a redraw.

## The ladder

Three stages gated on **runway** (`runwayStage`), which never drops once earned
(`activeStage`, plus `evidenceStage` so a returning user who already logged assets
keeps their tools). `STAGE_META` carries what each rung opens.

**Stage map** (`stageMapHTML`, `openStageMap`) - tapping the stage bar opens all
three rungs at once: what each one gives, whether it is unlocked, and a progress
bar toward the ones that are not. The ladder used to be invisible from below - you
could see "3 mo to Stage 2" but never what Stage 2 was, which makes a gate feel
arbitrary instead of earned.

**The gate stays runway, deliberately.** A "confirm I finished Stage 1" button was
considered and rejected: it would hand someone net worth, offense tracking and the
sovereignty audit while they still have no cushion, and every one of those tools
reads wrong without one. Settings keeps **Show everything** for anyone who wants
past the system entirely, and the map says so rather than pretending the gate is
the only option.

## Visual language

**Midnight** (dark, the default) is a deep blue ground carrying warm ochre
content - the pairing of a warm painting hung on a midnight wall. The app's first
palette was blue but COLD: blue-grey text on near-black navy, which read as a
trading terminal. The blue was never the problem; the temperature of everything
sitting on it was. Text is warm cream, the accent is soft gold, so the ground
stays blue and everything on it stays warm.

**Ledger** (light) is paper rather than white, because a budget is a ledger and a
ledger is paper. The `auto` theme setting picks per device.

The **expression layer** at the end of the stylesheet lowers the voice of the
whole interface in one place. Before it, the app carried 241 declarations at
weight 700+ against 33 at 600 or below, 42 `text-transform:uppercase` rules and
47 `letter-spacing` rules: nothing was quiet, so nothing had hierarchy, and every
micro-label read as instrument telemetry. Headings and prose use a **system serif
stack** (Iowan / Palatino / Georgia) - no font file, so "zero external requests"
stays literally true.

**Emoji are navigation only.** The app carried 116 distinct glyphs, which is the
single strongest machine-made signal: a person picks eight icons and reuses them.
336 decorative runs were removed; what remains is the tab bar and the theme
toggle. Two glyphs that turned out to be functional rather than decorative were
replaced with words or marks instead of restored - the Freedom Mode toggle now
reads `$ / hrs`, and the action button is a `+`.

Contrast is checked, not assumed: every foreground/background pair in both themes
meets WCAG AA or better. The light accent was darkened from `#9a6b2f` to
`#8a5f28` because the original sat at 4.06 on paper and 4.39 behind button text,
both short of AA for normal-size text.

## The flow (primary nav + More)

The bottom nav leads with the three core jobs and tucks the support surfaces behind
a **More** toggle, so a new user sees a sharp, low-clutter front door instead of seven
competing tabs. **Shield** (the anti-impulse differentiator) carries a gold accent.

**Bottom bar (thumb-reach, 5 slots):** Home · Plan · **[I want to...]** · Build · More
**More ▾ expands upward:** Shield · Track · Debt · Learn · Settings.

The nav moved from a top tab strip to a fixed bottom bar with a centre notch holding
the "I want to..." hub. Shield moved into More deliberately: its *action* (the
gut-check) is the hub's gold hero row, reachable in one tap from any screen, while the
Shield *tab* is the review surface (vault, War Chest, badges) you visit less often.
The bar is opaque with a top shadow, respects `env(safe-area-inset-bottom)`, and the
notch button lifts clear when More is expanded. Body padding compensates for both
states so nothing hides behind the bar.

The underlying money journey is unchanged: **Plan → Track → Shield → Build → Learn**
(shown as the numbered flow on Home). Internal view ids in parentheses are unchanged
for code stability; only the labels and grouping changed (`Defend` label → **Shield**).

## Cross-cutting systems

**Voice engine** - two axes drive all copy:
- **Register** (Gen Z / Middle / Mature) - set at the intake age-gate; 2026-appropriate vocabulary.
- **Intensity** (Clean / Blunt / Savage) - the **Settings > Voice & tone** dial (moved out of the header so it no longer rides above every screen); how much mercy. The rotating truth-quote hero (`.quote-wrap`) now shows on **Home only** (`body.on-home`), keeping the other tabs' headers lean.
- Pickers: `pickVoice` (register x intensity, floored by situation), `pickReg` (register), `pickTrapVoiceCtx`. Quote bank `QUOTES`, plus per-feature matrices (`IMP_*`, `TRAP_RESPONSES`, `CHALLENGE_STING`, `REBALANCE_COPY`, `BLINDSPOT_COPY`, `LIFE_LABELS`).
- **Tone safety lock** - sensitive/essential spend (rent, medical, utilities, groceries, emergencies, and any Cover First essential) forces the **Clean** register regardless of the dial, so the app never roasts someone over a hospital bill or their rent. `SENSITIVE_RX`, `isSensitive`, `effInt`; context-aware pickers `pickVoiceCtx` / `pickTrapVoiceCtx` power the rebalance banner and the impulse gut-check.

**Freedom Mode** (`$` / ⌛ header toggle) - converts every figure into **hours of your life** via `fmtLife` (minutes → hours → work-days → work-months). `money()` is mode-aware; `usd()` is always dollars. Needs an hourly wage. `renderFreedomToggle`, `renderAll`.

**The unit rule.** `money()` converting everything is right for a figure you *read* and wrong for two kinds it does not own, so **`actMoney()`** (always dollars) marks the exceptions:

1. **A figure you act on.** Every input takes dollars - the app says so itself, *"you always type dollars, only the display changes"* - so a button reading `Assign 3.2 days`, or an instruction reading `Set it to at least 1.6 days`, names a value the field will not accept. Same fault as offering to bank ten minutes into a goal: goals hold dollars, and nobody can deposit ten minutes.
2. **A figure whose own caption already converts.** The headline converts too and the card prints one amount twice in two units: *"Spent this month 20.3 days / That's 162 hrs of life"*.

Applies to button labels, typed-value instructions (`Set it to`, `Assign your last`, `You need at least`, `Give`), and any headline with a life aside beneath it. The **Debt planner** states its own version of the rule in its intro - *"the interest priced in hours of your life"* - so what you **pay** and what you **owe** stay dollars while what the debt **costs** carries the life framing. Locked by `tests/life_units.mjs`.

**True Net Hourly Wage** (Home card + Settings, one engine: `applyTrueRate`) - `(take-home - overhead) / (your work month + commute hrs)`. The work month comes from `state.hoursPerWeek` when the user gave it (hours/week x 52 / 12), else the full-time default `WORKMONTH_HRS` (2,080/12 = 173.33) - the app asks for real hours rather than assuming 40, and hours/week is editable in Settings next to the wage. The same real month scales `fmtLife`'s day/month buckets and the no-wage income fallback (`effectiveHourly`), so a 60-hour-week person's month of pay reads as "1.0 mo" of THEIR life. Feeds every hours-of-life figure. Stored in `state.hourlyWage`.

**The welcome gate** (`#iaWelcome`, `iaShowWelcome`) - a full-screen card the user reads and accepts before the setup chat asks anything. Ground zero (everybody starts at zero; zero is today, not a verdict), the distance between here and where they want to be, that this is an accountability app rather than a budgeting one, and three promises as their own cards - *I am listening*, *I am watching*, *You can do better*. Then the part most software will not say, styled apart from everything above it: **the app is not real**. It is a tool, it cannot want this for them, it cannot spend the money or refuse to, it cannot even waste their time because it has none to waste - so the effort is theirs, all of it. The CTA asks for work (*"I'm ready to work"*) rather than promising a result. Shown once, tracked by `welcomed`; a re-run of setup by somebody who already accepted goes straight to the questions. Full copy and mechanics in `docs/Budget-Intake.md`.

**Intake chat** - conversational onboarding; opens on first run or "Setup chat". Age-gate, then the tone dial (so the whole conversation speaks in the chosen voice), then the soul layer (situation / money story / budgeting history), then real data (income -> recurring, essentials -> Cover First, dream -> goal). See `docs/Budget-Intake.md`. `openIntake`, `INTAKE`, `commitIntake`.

**"Left to budget" is one number, computed one way.** Both Home and Plan use `topCats().reduce((s,c)=>s+catAssigned(c.id,M),0)` so each pool is counted exactly once - Home previously summed *every* category including children, which double-counted any parent that had subcategories and made the two tabs disagree about the same month. `ltbNoteHTML` renders the arithmetic in plain words under the cards on both screens, because a negative "left to budget" reads as failure when it usually just means income has not been logged yet: "You've given $2,510 a job but haven't logged any income yet... Nothing is overspent."

**The four traps are defined where they're asked about.** `TRAPS` carries three levels of wording: `label` (the name), `pick` (the plain-words one-liner shown inside the dropdown, e.g. "Scroll Trap - an ad or a feed put it in front of me") and `TRAP_MEANS` (the full explanation, each ending in a concrete "Tell:" so the user can self-identify). Both pickers - the Shield form and the trap-scan modal - are filled from that one source by `fillTrapPickers`, so the wording can never drift between them, and a "What are the four traps?" legend on Shield teaches the vocabulary once. Naming a concept the app invented without ever defining it is just jargon.

**Category nesting + two-way pool math.** Categories nest three levels: Getting Around → Gas → Car 1 / Car 2, or Food → Groceries → Walmart / Meijer (`descendantsOf`, `catDepth`, `catSpent` all recurse; `catName` renders the full breadcrumb). Level 3 is the floor - deeper stops being a plan and starts being filing - enforced by `catDepth` and by simply not offering a split affordance on level-3 rows.

The budgeting math runs in **both directions**, because people know their numbers at different levels. *Top-down:* assign $400 to Getting Around and split it into Gas and its cars - the note reads "$150 left to split into subs." *Bottom-up:* you know water is $70 and electric is $120 but have no idea what the utilities total is, so you fill in only the subs and the parent adds them up ("↑ $190 total, added up from your 2 subs", with a `= $190` roll tag beside the name, and an **Assign $190 →** button that writes the rolled-up figure into the parent's own field in one tap, turning the derived number into a committed one). `catAssigned(id,M)` returns `max(own, sum of children)` recursively, so filling in subs is **never** an error - the old "over-split the pool" warning is gone. Every total that used the flat figure (Left to budget, the summary, next-steps) now uses `catAssigned`. Auto-Rebalance only moves genuinely free money: a pool's dollars already committed to its own subs can't be raided (`freeOf` caps the take by both the pool's surplus and its uncommitted remainder).

**Accounts - what's actually in the bank** (`state.accounts`, Build tab). Everything else in the app is derived from logged transactions, which meant a new user with $8,000 in checking was told they had $0 - and net worth and Freedom Runway were understated to match. Accounts are the one surface where the user simply states the truth: name, kind (`ACCT_KINDS` - checking/savings/cash/investment/other, each flagged liquid or not) and an earmark (`ACCT_PURPOSES` - emergency, college, sinking, a dream, retirement). `bankTotal` feeds net worth, `liquidTotal` feeds the runway (falling back to the transaction-derived figure only when no accounts exist), and `earmarked(purpose)` is what turns "savings" into answerable questions - how much is emergency money, how much is the kids'. Balances are never silently edited: editing one restamps its `updated` date, and `loggedSince` shows the drift between what has been logged and what the user last said was real, leaving the reconciliation to them. The Track tab's transaction-derived figure is relabelled "Logged net (all time)" once accounts exist, with a note explaining it is not a bank balance.

**Dig deeper (after the four walls).** Covering the essentials used to end in a dead-end congratulation card. It is replaced by a ladder (`deepenSteps`, `deepenHTML`, `runDeepen`) that hands the user the next real move at the moment they are most engaged, as one sequenced ladder: **$1,000 between you and disaster** -> **the membership sweep** (a quick win that funds the rest) -> **kill the interest** (done only when balances are entered AND cleared) -> **grow it to three months of essentials** -> **pay yourself first, every month** -> **fund what's coming for the kids** -> **own where you live outright** (hidden unless a mortgage liability exists) -> **build it, and let it move** (giving). The order is the widely-taught one because it works, written in the app's own words rather than branded as anyone's program, and every rung reads the user's real numbers (`emergencyMoney` combines earmarked account balances with matching goal progress; `debtsOutstanding`, `hasMortgage`) instead of asking them to self-report. The savings rungs create and focus a real earmarked account rather than a wishful goal. Each step self-detects whether it is already done (`payFirstDone`, `emergencyDone`, `state.subSweep`, `state.debts`), any step can be dismissed with "Not now" (`state.deepenSkipped`), and the ladder shows all four as chips so the user sees the whole path. It appears both under the funded wall on Plan and as a one-line hand-off on the Home walls grid.

**Membership sweep** (`SUB_SEEDS`, `renderSubSweep`, `openSubSweep`; `#subSweepPanel` on Plan, hidden until opened). Twelve generic membership categories as one-tap chips (no brand pricing - the user knows their own numbers), each row showing its annual cost as it is typed, then a total priced in hours of life ("$75/mo is 45 hrs of your life every year, on autopilot"). "Add these to my budget" writes them as **Memberships > each service** using the nested categories, so they roll up into one pool. The copy is explicitly non-judgmental: a membership you use and love is money well spent; the point is knowing which ones those are. Recorded in `state.subSweep`.

Skips are **month-scoped** (`deepenSkipMonth`): "Not now" sets a step aside for the current month only, and the close card never claims a dismissed step was done - it names what was set aside and offers a way back.

**Recurring schedules.** Bills and paychecks don't all run on a day-of-the-month, so each item carries a **frequency** (weekly / every 2 weeks / twice a month / monthly / every 3 months / once a year) plus an **anchor date** - the first time it's due. `recOccurrences(r, month)` works out every date the item lands on inside a given month (clamping to short months, so a 31st anchor bills Feb 28), and `postRecurring` dedupes per **occurrence** rather than per month, so a weekly bill posts four or five times instead of once. Adding a recurring item whose first date has already arrived posts it immediately, rather than leaving a just-created item looking like it did nothing until the next app launch. For the **current** month only, `postRecurring` stops at today, so "Spent" stays a fact rather than a forecast (a weekly bill doesn't book the whole month on the 3rd); past and future months post in full, and boot sweeps last month before this one so nothing is missed by not opening the app. `recNextDue` drives the "next Aug 21" label, and `recMonthly` gives the average-month equivalent shown beside non-monthly items (≈$3,250/mo for a $1,500 bi-weekly paycheck). That same normalization feeds `recomputeBlendedWage`, so a bi-weekly paycheck no longer reads as a monthly one. Legacy items that only carried `day` keep their old behavior exactly: monthly, always due, postable to any month. `REC_FREQS`, `recOccurrences`, `postRecurring`.

**Cover First** (the Four Walls) - Roof / Food / Power & Wi-Fi / Getting Around, matched to categories by keyword (`WALLS`). Drives the Home grid, essential-runway, and sovereignty. Tapping a wall deep-links to its category on Plan and starts the **wall-to-wall guide** (`wallGuideCat`, `wallGuideHTML`): fund it and an inline banner hands you the next uncovered wall, ending in an all-covered close - no bouncing back to Home between essentials. On the Home grid, covered walls collapse to compact ✓ chips (still tappable to adjust) and the whole grid folds to one line when all four are covered; walls are per-month, so they return asking when the month turns. `renderWalls`, `goToWall`, `findOrCreateEssential`.

**Money map** (`renderBreakdown`, `bdRows`, `bdDonutSVG`) - a donut of where money
went and where it came from, over a **1 / 3 / 6 / 12 month** window. A single month
is a snapshot and a snapshot lies: one car repair makes Getting Around look like a
habit, so the interval is a first-class control rather than something faked by
clicking the month arrows twelve times. Expenses drill into the category tree
(Food → Groceries → Walmart) and money booked straight to a parent shows as
"(direct)" so nothing vanishes on the way down. Income groups by source and does
not drill. Slices are `stroke-dasharray` on one circle each: no library, no canvas.

**Quick glance** (`renderGlance`, `glanceReady`) - an edge pull-tab opening money
in, money out, and what that leaves for the active month, plus today's spend
against the daily allowance in spending mode. Gated on `genuineTxDates`, the same
predicate the calendar origin uses, so an auto-posted paycheck does not count as
"tracking has started" and nobody meets a drawer reading $0 / $0 / $0 on day one.

**Silent Sovereignty Audit** - Sovereign Capital Ratio, Overhead Drag, Pure Freedom Runway, and a 4-tier classification (Encumbered → Tethered → Unbound → Untouchable). The rank is deliberately NOT called "Sovereign": that name belongs to the paid sync tier, and a free rank sharing it reads as a paywall. `sovereignty`, `renderSovereignty`.

**Anti-shame** - overspend shows a calm **Rebalance Banner** (never red errors); the **Blindspot Shield** rewards logging. Copy never says failed/bad/violated/ruined.

**Progressive interface** - the app unrolls depth as the user's Freedom Runway grows, so a user in survival mode is not buried in investment tools:
- **Stage 1 Defense** (<3 mo runway): Cover First + Shield only. Build tab and the offense/mindset tools are hidden.
- **Stage 2 Expansion** (3-12 mo): Build tab, Net Worth, Offense vs. Defense, Skills, and the Enough anchor unroll.
- **Stage 3 Autonomy & Leverage** (12 mo+): the Sovereignty Audit and Network Capital open.
- **One-way ratchet**: tools never disappear once earned (`stageReached`); crossing a threshold fires a **milestone celebration** (`celebrateStage`). Panels/tabs are gated by a `data-stage` attribute; `.stage-locked` hides anything above the active stage.
- **Override**: Settings > Interface depth toggles `uiMode` between `auto` (guided) and `all` (show everything). Existing users are migrated so they never lose a tool they already used (`evidenceStage`).
- **Retrospective / timeline** (`renderRetro`): auto-captured monthly snapshots (`captureSnapshot` upserts the current month on boot; past months freeze) drive a **Monthly/Quarterly** net-worth timeline with **3M / 6M / 1Y / All / Custom** date ranges, a start-vs-now delta, and a period table. `metricSnapshot`, `retroSeries`, `quarterKey`; state in `snapshots` + `retroView`. Engine: `runwayStage`, `activeStage`, `applyStage`, `STAGE_META`.

**PWA** - installable, offline-capable: `manifest.webmanifest` (with a "Scan a trap" long-press shortcut), `sw.js` (network-first page, cache-first assets), `icon*.png` / `icon.svg`. `initPWA`.

**CSV import** (Track tab) - drag/drop a bank statement; on-device parse with column auto-detection and category auto-guess, review-before-commit. `parseCSV`, `detectCols`, `extractRows`, `guessCat`, `commitImport`.

**Encrypted backup** (Settings) - WebCrypto AES-GCM + PBKDF2 to a portable `.acct` file; restore replaces local state. `encryptBackup`, `restoreBackup`, `renderBackupNote`.

**Sovereign tier - Household Sync (Blind Vault, E2EE)** - opt-in cloud sync for couples that never lets the server read financial data. Config-gated: off until `SUPABASE_URL` / `SUPABASE_ANON_KEY` are set (the free tier never loads the SDK - `getSupabase` lazy-loads it only on use). The whole `state` object is AES-256-GCM encrypted on-device with a user-only **Sync Passphrase** (`encryptVault`/`decryptVault`, PBKDF2 210k); only the ciphertext envelope is stored in Supabase's single `user_vaults` table (`{user_id, encrypted_payload}`, RLS = own row only). The passphrase lives **in memory only** (`syncPass`), never localStorage, so a fresh session re-asks for it. Engine: `sbSignUp/sbSignIn/sbSignOut/sbSession`, `pushToVault`, `pullFromVault` (wrong passphrase → "Invalid Sync Passphrase. We cannot reset this for you."), and a debounced auto-push wired into `save()` (`scheduleVaultPush`). UI in the Household panel (`renderSync`, `#syncPanel`): a Host flow (create account → create passphrase behind a blunt *"we cannot reset it"* warning → `pushToVault` → Vault Active) and a Joiner flow (email + password + passphrase → `signIn` + `pullFromVault`). The vault is the shared source of truth; last write wins (fine for a couple). **Configured, and still not verifiable from the build environment.** The project is live and the publishable key is in the file (both values are public by design - RLS is what protects the data, and a static HTML app has nowhere else to put them). What cannot be done here is prove it works: this session's network policy refuses `*.supabase.co` and `cdn.jsdelivr.net`, so no sign-up, push or pull has ever been executed. **A human has to run the two-profile round trip** - see `tests/USER-TESTING.md`. The paid gate (Stripe → entitlement → RLS) is a later, separate step; the client lock is cosmetic until then.

*Configuring the keys immediately broke a promise that had been true only because they were placeholders.* The comment above says the free tier fetches nothing until sync is explicitly started - and `refreshSyncState()` checked for a Supabase session on **every boot for anyone with Household mode on**, which fetches the SDK from a CDN. Turning on Household mode means *two of us share this budget on this phone*; it says nothing about a server. So every household user would have made an uninvited third-party request on a page whose headline is that nothing leaves the device, and a failed fetch surfaced as an uncaught `Could not load the sync engine` page error rather than a sentence on screen. The network is now touched only once somebody has actually created or joined a vault (`state.syncOptIn`), and an unreachable service is a state rather than a crash. Guarded by the `syncgate` probe, which asserts **zero network requests on load** with Household on.

**Debt Payoff Planner** (`Debt` tab, in More) - a modernized snowball/avalanche calculator. List debts (name, balance, APR, min payment) or import from liabilities; set a monthly amount; pick **Avalanche** (highest APR first, cheapest) or **Snowball** (lowest balance first, quick wins). Shows debt-free date, total interest **priced in hours of your life**, a payoff-order list, a balance timeline, and a live comparison of how much the chosen strategy saves vs. the other. Simulation validated against the Vertex42 calculator to the penny. `simulateDebts`, `renderDebt`, `renderDebtResults`. Keys: `debts`, `debtBudget`, `debtStrategy`.
Plus a **"Plot twist: invest instead?"** panel - the honest invest-vs-payoff comparison. Same money out each month, run over 5/10/20 years: **Crush the debt first** vs **Minimums + invest the rest** at an expected market return, compared by **what is left of that one decision** - the invested pot minus what is still owed on the listed debts - with per-debt flags (debt APR below the return -> investing likely wins; above -> crush it, guaranteed return) and a guaranteed-vs-expected/emergency-fund/match/not-advice caveat. `investCompare`, `renderInvestCompare`. Keys: `investReturn`, `investYears`. See **A partial number wearing a whole number's name** below for why the two cards no longer say *net worth*.

**Borrowing to build** (`Debt` tab, below the invest comparison) - the leverage tool. It came out of a direct question: how does the app view a HELOC or a low-rate card sitting in the Build column, and is the philosophy to forbid them or to use them? Neither. The app has never had a position on borrowing - rung 3 of the Growth Ladder says *kill the interest*, which is about **rate**, not about debt existing. What it could not do was tell a 6% line from a 26% card, or price a borrowing against the thing it funds.

You give it the amount, the rate you pay, what you expect the thing to return, and a horizon (3/5/10/20 yrs). Optionally the payment you must make and the cash it actually hands you. It answers with:

- **The break-even return**, as the headline: what the thing has to return every year just to stand still. On a carried balance that lands exactly on the rate, at every horizon, which is the cleanest way to say what a borrowing costs. Supply a payment and the balance amortizes, the interest total falls, and the break-even falls with it.
- **Two outcome cards, always both.** What you make if the guess is right, and what it costs you if it returns nothing - which is the interest, whatever the guess was, plus a 20% drawdown line beside it. There is no code path that prints one without the other; a leverage screen showing only the upside would be the most dangerous surface in the app.
- **The servicing gap.** Payment minus the cash it hands you is money leaving your own income every month, return or no return, sized against your essentials. When the cash covers the payment it says that too, and names the condition (as long as the cash keeps arriving).
- **The underwater flag.** A payment below the monthly interest is not a payment, it is a subscription to a growing balance - it says so and shows what you would owe at the end.
- **The buffer check.** Under three months of Freedom Runway and it says plainly that a bad stretch forces the sale, and a forced sale is where the loss gets locked in. Otherwise, if `riskStanding()` shows uncovered events, it names them as the number to hold the payment up against.
- **Its working**, behind the `?` on the headline, quoting live numbers through both the compounding and the amortized case.

Deliberately **not** stage-gated. The first draft gated it at stage 3, which hides it from everyone except people with a year of runway - the group that least needs the warning - and would have made the under-three-months warning unreachable, since stage 2 already requires three. A warning that can never fire is not a warning.

The door: a liability with a rate at or under 10% is named as cheap money on the Build tab and offered the `leverage` trail button. A 26% store card is not called a lever.

The one thing it never does is say whether to borrow, and it says so out loud. `leverageMath`, `levVerdict`, `renderLeverage`, `FIELD_WHY.levBreak`, `VOICE.levNeg/levThin/levWide/levGap/levBare`. Key: `lev` (`amt`, `apr`, `ret`, `years`, `pay`, `cash` - `pay` and `cash` stored monthly, entered in any period; a blank expected return is stored as `null` so "I expect nothing" and "I have not said" stay different answers).

**Borrow it or wait for it** (`Debt` tab, below the leverage tool) - asked for as *"I would like to have my credit limit included with the debt calculator so it can start tracking ways to leverage vs dreams and goals"*, sent with a HELOC at 3.49% on screen and nothing drawn on it. Two asks in one sentence, and the first exposed a blocker: **the planner would not take the row at all.** It demanded a balance above zero, so a line at zero was rejected as an empty entry - when a line at zero is not empty, it is the cheapest money that person has and the only debt on the list that could fund something tomorrow. A balance of nothing is now a real answer *as long as a limit came with it*; without one it is still refused, because a $0 car loan is a typo.

A **limit is what makes a debt revolving**, and its presence is the whole declaration - nothing extra to tick. A car loan cannot lend back what you have repaid; a card and a line can. `debtLimit`, `debtRoom`, `debtUtil` mirror the account-side helpers, the row grows a second line (room left, utilisation band, and the one question that changes what borrowing means: *is something you own behind this?*) only when a limit exists, and clearing the box **removes** the property rather than storing a `0` - the absence is the statement, and a stored zero would leave a car loan claiming to be a line with no room. `pricedLines()` now takes `room`, `secured` and `revolving` from debts as well as accounts, so a HELOC typed into the planner reaches the rate signals that previously only saw credit accounts. Same fact, two doors.

The second ask is the panel. It is **not** the leverage tool and says so: leverage prices a rate you pay against a return you hope for, and that arithmetic sometimes comes out ahead. **A dream returns nothing** - a kitchen pays no dividend, a holiday never comes back - so there is no break-even to clear and no version where borrowing wins on the money. What it buys is *time*. So the comparison holds the **monthly amount fixed**, which is the only way the two halves are comparable: put that money aside and you get the thing in N months, or borrow and get it today and pay for longer. The months are what you bought, the interest is the price, priced also in hours of a life. `borrowOrWait` refuses to loop when the payment is under the first month's interest, the way the leverage tool already flags it.

The figure that would have been quietly wrong: the first draft quoted the **cheapest** line's rate over the **total** room, so a $10,000 dream funded $3,000 from a 3.49% line and $7,000 from a 23.9% card came out priced at 3.49%. True of part of the money, presented as true of all of it - the exact fault the rate signals exist to refuse. `drawRoom()` fills cheapest-line-first and returns the **blend of what was actually taken**, names both lines when there is more than one, and a dream bigger than the room says so instead of pricing a fantasy.

Everything else on the panel is a guardrail rather than a feature: the room is called **not money and not net worth** in the sentence after it is stated; **utilisation** is tracked against the 30% and 70% bands because it is the number nobody sees until it has moved; a **secured** line names what is standing behind it, since there is no interest rate for losing the thing; and the runway check fires the same way the leverage tool's does, because room on a line is also what catches a boiler. Gated on **room, not on debts** - two loans and no line leave it waiting however much is owed, and one untouched HELOC with no other debt opens it, which is exactly who asked. `renderRoom`, `roomLines`, `roomTotal`, `roomUsed`, `cheapestRoom`, `unpricedRoom`, `drawRoom`, `borrowOrWait`, `goalGap`, `goalPace`, `VOICE.roomBuy/roomCheap/roomSecured/roomWait`. Keys: `debts[].limit`, `debts[].secured`, `roomPay` (stored monthly, entered in any period; blank falls back per dream to the pace its own **date** already demands).

**The same line, on both sides** - asked for immediately after the planner got limits: *"Now add my HELOC limit to the accounts side too."* The accounts side already held a limit, a rate and a collateral flag - but under the label **"Credit card / line of credit"**, with a placeholder of `13700`, a collateral option reading *"Backed by something I own"*, and a summary that opened every line with **"On the cards:"**. Every one of those was accurate and not one of them was the word on the person's paperwork. Someone scanning a dropdown for their product does not translate; they conclude it is not there. The kind is now **"Credit card, HELOC or line of credit"**, the collateral option names a home, the placeholder describes the number instead of guessing its size, and the summary words itself from what is actually there - naming the line when it is a line, splitting cards from lines when there are both, and saying which part of the room sits behind something you own, because a card's room and a HELOC's room are not the same kind of money.

The crossing itself is a **one-tap offer with the reason attached**, not a silent copy, because the two sides do different jobs: the planner works out the payoff order, the date and the interest; the accounts side is what **net worth** counts and the only figure in the app that comes from outside your own typing. `netWorth()` reads accounts and liabilities and has never read `state.debts`, and `pricedLines()` dedupes by name - so a line living in both places moves net worth **exactly once**, appears in the rate layer **once**, and its room is counted **once**. All three are asserted, because they are precisely the things that would be silently wrong. The record carries balance (flipped negative, the one conversion the credit model rests on), limit, rate and the secured flag, and gets a first reading dated today.

What two sides *can* do is **drift**, and that is named rather than synced away. When a planner debt and a credit account share a name and disagree on the balance, the panel prints both figures and says which side drives what - net worth reads the Build figure, the payoff date reads the planner's - and refuses to overwrite either, because the accounts figure is a reading off a statement and quietly replacing it with something typed would destroy the one property that makes that side worth anything. A payoff date built on a stale balance is worse than no date, because it looks finished. The reverse trail exists too: the accounts summary offers **"Price it against a dream"** through to the room panel (`TRAILS.room`). `roomToAccts`, `TRAILS.room`, `ACCT_KINDS` credit label.

**Every figure moved; nothing said so** (`acctHistHTML`, the migration backfill, the no-ledger toast) - reported as *"My overflow income didn't change or statistics when I changed my balance. Are they all being tracked?"*, with a screenshot where one account carried a full block (expected balance, entries since, show-the-work) and the one below it showed a date and nothing else. **They were all being tracked.** The balance, the bank total, net worth, the month's snapshot, the trend and a stored reading all moved on that keystroke, and not one of them said so. Two causes, both silence: the account had no ledger filed against it, so the reconcile branch stayed quiet by design; and the reading history returned empty under two readings, while an account added before readings existed started at **zero** of them - so the first change produced a history of one, which rendered as nothing, which is indistinguishable from not being tracked at all.

Three fixes. The migration **backfills the reading that was already there** - an account carrying a balance and the date it was read has a reading, two stored facts never converted into the shape that can show them, so writing it down restates rather than invents. `acctHistHTML` now renders a **history of one** (*"1 reading, taken 2026-08-20"*) instead of nothing, because a history of one is still proof it was written down and that account is the case where the reading is the only evidence. And typing a balance into an account with nothing logged against it now **answers**, naming the move, the date it moved from, and the fact that the month's figures were reworked from it - including when it did not move, because a reading that is flat is still a reading and the trend needs the flat months or they read as gaps.

**The planning calendar** (`Plan` tab, above the membership sweep) - asked for as *"a planning calendar which holds all of my reoccurring payments and income as a visual.. With a simple check box that says did it land in my account.. So it can be added to My tracker"*. The recurring list is a list of **rules**: it can say a bill repeats monthly and it can never say the **shape** of the month - which week is heavy, whether the paycheck lands before the rent or four days after it. A month grid of every occurrence, dotted by kind (in / out / invested) and **filled once it has landed**, with the month totalled both directions and a running *"2 of 6 have landed"*. Tapping a day jumps to it in the list below.

The checkbox keeps **no state of its own**, and that is the design. An occurrence has landed exactly when a transaction exists carrying that rule's id and that date - the same test `postRecurring` already uses to avoid posting twice - so the box **reads the tracker** rather than holding a second opinion about it, and the two cannot drift. Tick posts the entry (identical shape to what the scheduler writes, carrying rule, category and account); untick removes only what the rule created, never a hand-logged entry, and never touches the rule - reversible in one tap, which is why it is not the kind of delete this app arms.

Built wrong first, and the fault is worth keeping: the box sat on top of an engine that **already auto-posted everything**, so every past occurrence arrived pre-ticked and it confirmed nothing. A tick that is always already there is decoration. So **"Wait for me to tick it"** is a choice on the panel, defaulted **off** so no existing install changes behaviour - and for most bills auto-posting is right, since the direct debit left whether or not anyone opened the app. What it buys the person who turns it on is the thing they asked for: **a paycheck that never arrived is visible.** Turning it on never un-logs anything; turning it off catches up what was due rather than leaving a hole. The manual *Post recurring* button bypasses it either way, because that is somebody asking out loud rather than the app deciding. `calOccurrences`, `calMonthSums`, `calPost`, `calUnpost`, `calConfirmOn`, `renderCalendar` (owned solely by `renderRecurring`, so every path that changes a rule or the month redraws it once). Key: `calConfirm`.

**A face for every category** (`CAT_EMOJI`, `guessCatEmoji`, `catEmoji`, `catFaceOf`) - asked for as *"can each category get its own emoji before the title to give it a bit more personality on an overall very boring website"*, sent with nine categories rendered as nine identical beige rows. The same report as *"everything feels like a big run-on sentence"*, arriving on a different screen.

It **guesses**, which is the decision that makes it work. A picker on every category is a chore nobody finishes, and three of twelve decorated looks broken rather than personal - so the name is matched against an ordered keyword list and a category has a face the moment it exists, with no typing ever. Order is the whole design: anything specific sits above the generic word inside it, so *car insurance* beats *insurance* and *pet food* beats *food*. A name matching nothing still gets one, chosen by **kind** rather than a single generic - money saved, invested, given and paid down already read differently everywhere else and get faces that agree.

And the guess is **derived, not stored**. Rename "Food" to "Groceries" and the plate becomes a trolley, because nothing was frozen at creation. Only an explicit pick is written to the category, and a later rename must then leave it alone - the pair of assertions that would have caught storing the guess. **Planning ahead** (`calPrev` / `calNext` / `calNow`) - *"I'm not seeing how to plan ahead for future months? What good is a plan if you can't see what you are planning for."* The fault was purely navigational: `recOccurrences` has always answered for any month, and the Plan tab's month arrows sit at the very top of a long page, four panels above the calendar. **A month view with no way to change the month is a calendar of one month.** The calendar carries its own arrows now, and moving them moves the whole Plan tab, the way the arrows at the top always did.

**A colour per place** (`body[data-view]`, `--view`) - asked for as *"the website is completely monotone. Would it be better to have each section present a particular colour so people can easily see where they're landing? We can still fit in with the theme that we have set up but each page has its own distinct colour. **Almost transparent**."*

"Almost transparent" is the whole brief and it is the right instinct. An app that changes colour boldly between tabs stops looking like one app; what is wanted is walking into a different room in the same house. So every tint is a **wash** at low alpha over the existing surfaces, and the only places the hue appears at full strength are the rule above a heading and the bar over the active tab - neither of which carries text. **Two triples per view**, because the palette already inverts by theme and a hue picked to glow on navy is invisible on warm paper. Guarded in `palette.mjs` rather than the structure suite, because the failure that matters is a contrast one: the wash sits under every word on the page, so the suite **composites** panel + tint at the real alpha and re-checks every ink against it (290 pairs). **"Just my spending" mode** - opt-in in Settings for the partner who doesn't run the bills. **New-month plan prompt** (`#newMonthBanner`, `newMonthPromptHTML`) - a fresh month opens as blank assign boxes, and the `Copy last month` button sits in a toolbar nobody reads, so people retyped a whole budget or quietly stopped budgeting. When the active month is genuinely untouched **and** the previous month has a plan, the month itself offers to carry it forward: it names last month's total and category count, says how that lands against the income logged so far (matches to the dollar / leaves $X unassigned / is $X more than has come in), and offers `Copy <month> and edit it` or `Start from scratch`. **Repeats live on the row** (`repeatCtrlHTML`, `wireRepeat`, `recForCat`, `ROW_FREQS`) - setting up a recurring bill meant leaving the category you were looking at, scrolling to a separate section, and retyping its name, its amount and its category: three facts already on screen. Every **leaf** category now carries a `↻` checkbox; ticking it creates the recurring item from the row (amount seeded from that month's assignment, monthly by default, anchored to the 1st) and reveals a frequency select offering **weekly / every 2 weeks / monthly / every 3 months**. Unticking removes the template and leaves anything already posted alone. The Recurring panel stays for income and anything that is not a category, and **its form now autofills the amount** from the chosen category's assignment - never overwriting a figure someone typed. `.sub-name` gained a `min-width` floor and wraps rather than ellipsing: once the toggle joined the assign box, split and delete on one row, "Internet" was rendering as `"I..."`.

**Sheets fit the phone they open on** (`.modal`, `.modal.sheet`) - every modal had no `max-height` and no `overflow`, so it grew to whatever its content wanted. The app map wants 938px; on a 780px phone the overlay bottom-aligns the sheet, so the extra 158px went off the **top** and put the modal's own ✕ at `y=-115`. A sheet you cannot close is the worst state in an app, and it was on all of them - map, ladder, freedom explainer, talk-through, gut-check. `.modal` is now a flex column capped at `88dvh` (`88vh` fallback) with its last child scrolling, so the heading and the ✕ stay pinned where a thumb expects them. **The intake asks what you HAVE** (`haveNow` step, `state.accounts`) - it asked income, the four walls, other expenses and **debt**, and never once asked for a balance. So a full-budget user who declared debt finished setup with a net worth of exactly **minus their debt**: the app telling someone with $8,000 in the bank that they were $5,000 in the hole, because it had only ever heard one side of the ledger. It also left the runway unknown and the savings-context card permanently silent. One optional question after the debt questions - deliberately **one number**, since the split between current account and savings is what the Accounts panel on Build is for - and net worth reads +$3,000 instead of -$5,000. It rewords itself when a debt was just given ("That is one side of it. The other side...") and is skipped entirely on the light spending path. `tests/intake_cost.mjs` is exact by design and caught the promise going stale the moment the step landed: the full path's advertised count went 22 to 23 questions, in the test's `CLAIMS` **and** in the sentence the app says out loud.

**Who changed what, and when** (`SYNC_COLLECTIONS`, `stampChanges`, `mergeVault`, `changeLogHTML`, `syncCheck`) - two questions arrived together: *"why is pressing the sync button necessary, it feels like a hindrance, why doesn't it just update in real time?"* and *"will there be a change log so we know who changed what and when, so all account balances are always accurate?"* They have the same answer, and it was worse than either question assumed.

**Sending was already automatic** - `save()` has always scheduled a debounced push. The button did early what was going to happen anyway. **Receiving could not be**, because `pushToVault` sent the whole state as one blob and `pullFromVault` **replaced** the whole local state with it. `updated_at` was written to the row from the first day and never once read; there was no conflict detection of any kind. So: Pat logs a coffee at 9:00 and it goes up. Kristi edits a balance at 9:01 and *her* whole state goes up - a state that never contained the coffee. The vault now holds her copy, and when Pat's phone next pulls, the coffee is gone from the phone too. Silently. **Turning that into real time would have made an occasional loss a constant one** - the button was the only thing limiting the blast radius.

The fix and the change log are the same mechanism. Every item in a synced collection carries `_m` = *when it last changed, which partner changed it, on which device*, and merging is item by item rather than blob over blob. Rather than rewrite several hundred mutation sites, the stamp is **derived by diffing against the previous save** - one place, no caller can forget, and it cannot drift from what actually happened (the same argument that put `applyPanelGates` inside `save()`). Deletions leave **tombstones**, because a merge that resurrects something you deleted is the one failure nobody forgives - though an edit made *after* the delete does bring it back, since that is somebody deciding on purpose. Same-millisecond writes tie-break on device id, identically on both phones; without that, two devices can disagree forever about which copy is current. Settings merge per key on the same stamps, so a preference changed on one phone is not undone by an older copy from the other.

The change log is that same record read forwards: *"Kristi changed account Chequing · $2,600 - just now"*. Both phones' logs merge into one, newest first, deduplicated by entry id.

**And now the button is not load-bearing.** Receiving polls on a 15s interval (never while the tab is hidden), on tab focus, and on coming back online - and it reads the `updated_at` timestamp first, so the usual case of nothing having changed costs one small row read rather than pulling and decrypting a whole budget on a timer. The button reads *"Check for theirs now"*, because a button claiming to **do** the syncing implies the syncing does not happen without it.

`mergeVault` is deliberately **pure**, which is why the whole correctness story is provable without a network - the `syncmerge` probe asserts the old replace-behaviour loses the coffee, and that the merge keeps both sides. What remains unverified from the build environment is the round trip against the real project; that is still a human's job.


**Has this phone actually sent anything** (`syncTrafficHTML`, `state.syncLastPush` / `syncLastPull`) - three rounds of *"it still did not load"* went by with neither side able to tell whether a device had ever pushed. `syncNote` said *"Synced just now"* and vanished; the panel said **Vault active** whether or not a byte had moved.

A vault holds only **what was last successfully pushed to it**. A phone that has been locked since the change was made never sent it - `scheduleVaultPush` returns on its first line without a passphrase - so the other person pulls a vault that genuinely does not contain the thing they are looking for, which is indistinguishable from a broken merge. The Household panel now separates those two stories: *"Nothing from this phone has reached the vault yet - if your partner is missing something you added here, this is why"*, or once it has, **Last sent** and **Last received**.

**The category arrived; the money did not** (`SYNC_CELLMAPS`, `flatCells`, `setCell`, `state.cellM`) - reported as *"passphrase worked but it still did not load Sam's Club into the budget"*, which was the fourth round on the same complaint and the first one that was not a locked vault.

It was not the transport and it was not the passphrase. `categories` is a synced collection, so the category itself merged item by item and did arrive. The **assignment** did not. `budgets` is `{month: {category: amount}}` - every dollar decision for every month in one field - and `opening` is `{month: amount}`. Both sat in `SYNC_SETTINGS`, which merges per key: one stamp for the whole object, newest wins, loser discarded entire. So the moment both partners assigned money in the same month, one of them lost **every** assignment they had made, without a word. What the other person saw was a category that came across empty, which looks exactly like sync failing to deliver it.

The stamp now sits at the cell, not the field. `flatCells` flattens each map to its leaves (`budgets|2026-09|c1`), `stampChanges` stamps only the leaves that actually moved, and `mergeVault` reconciles cell by cell. Three consequences worth stating: an **absent** cell is a real value, so clearing an assignment beats a stale copy of it rather than being quietly restored; when neither side has ever stamped a cell - a budget that predates syncing - whichever side actually has it wins, so old budgets travel instead of being dropped; and the merge is symmetric, so both phones land on the same numbers regardless of which one merges. The `cellmerge` probe runs the Sam's Club scenario end to end and fails on the previous build at exactly the line the user reported.

*A real bug fell out of building it.* `pushToVault` wrote a fresh `updated_at` and never recorded it, so the next poll read a timestamp **it had moved itself**, concluded the other phone had news, and pulled back its own write every fifteen seconds forever. Harmless to the data, because merging is idempotent, and pure noise on the wire. The push records its own stamp now, asserted both ways: no self-pull, and a genuine change from the other side still comes down.


**Unlock where you are standing** (the inline form in `syncLockedHTML`, `refreshSyncState` on tab arrival) - reported immediately after the banner shipped: *"every time I press unlock sync it navigates to the Build tab, but when I go back to Plan I have to press it again."* Two faults in one tap.

The button walked to the Household panel, and **`refreshSyncState` was called at boot and nowhere else** - so arriving there showed whatever the panel had last drawn, frequently with no passphrase box in it at all. The Tripwires lesson for the third time: *a render function no surface calls is a panel that does not exist*, and this file's own breadcrumb walked straight into it. Arriving at that tab refreshes it now.

The better half of the fix is that **the journey should not exist**. Fixing something on Plan should not require leaving Plan, so the passphrase field lives inside the warning: type it, press Unlock, and the warning disappears underneath you without the tab ever changing. An empty box says what is wanted rather than navigating; a wrong passphrase explains itself in place and stays locked; Enter submits, because a password field that ignores it reads as broken.

*The layout suite then caught the build stamp beside it: `word-break:break-all` chopped "unlock-in-pla / ce" across two lines at 320px. A stamp exists to be read out loud, and one that breaks mid-word cannot be. `overflow-wrap:anywhere` breaks at the hyphens and spaces first.*


**A new flag reads as "no" for everybody already here** (the `syncOptIn` backfill in `load()`, `BUILD_STAMP`) - reported as *"no banner and no Sam's Club"*, which falsified the previous diagnosis and pointed at a regression this file had caused two commits earlier.

`syncOptIn` was added to stop sync fetching the SDK for people who had only turned on Household mode. It gates the session check, the unlock prompt, the passphrase restore **and** the warning banner - and an install that joined a vault *before the field existed* has no such key, so it defaulted to `false`. The app therefore concluded they had never opted in: it showed the **setup menu to somebody already signed in**, synced nothing in either direction, and displayed no warning, because the warning was gated behind the same broken flag. The person on the other phone had no way to diagnose any of it.

The evidence needed was already on the device and required no network: Supabase keeps its session under `sb-<project>-auth-token` in localStorage, and that key exists only if somebody signed in. It is backfilled **only when the field is truly absent** - an explicit `false` is somebody who signed out, and is left alone. The rule this leaves: **a new flag that gates existing behaviour must be asked what it reads as for the people who are already here.** This one read as *"you never set this up."*

**And the app can now say which build it is** (Settings → *Which version is this*). Two rounds of this loop went by with neither side able to say whether the phone was running the code being discussed. A deploy here is a static file with no build step, so the stamp is set by hand in the same commit as the change it names - *"2026-09-02 · sync-optin-fix"* answers "has the fix reached me" in a way a version number never does.


**A locked vault does nothing, in both directions** (`syncLockedHTML`, `syncPassRemember`, sessionStorage) - found by a real two-phone test: a subcategory added on one phone never reached the other. It was not the merge. The Sync Passphrase lived in a variable and nowhere else, so **every page load started with no key**: `syncActive` false, which meant `syncPollStart()` never ran (nothing received) and `scheduleVaultPush()` returned on its first line (**nothing sent either**). A phone discards its tab constantly, so that was the normal state rather than the rare one, and two phones sat diverging while both looked synced. The only thing that knew was an unlock box inside a panel on another tab.

**It says so where the budget is now.** A banner on Plan and Track names both directions (*"nothing you change here reaches Pat, and nothing of theirs reaches you"*), names the partner, explains why it locks so it does not read as a fault, and unlocks in one tap. Somebody who never started sync is not nagged about it.

**And the passphrase survives a reload**, in `sessionStorage`. Against the threat the memory-only choice was written for this changes nothing - the browser drops it on close, and Supabase still never sees it, so *even we cannot read your money* is untouched; against a script already running on the page it is no worse than the variable was. `localStorage` was deliberately not chosen: a key resting on a device indefinitely is exactly the trade the blind vault exists to avoid making on somebody's behalf. A remembered passphrase is treated as a **claim rather than a fact** - it is used by actually opening the vault with it, and a stale one is discarded for the unlock prompt rather than left to fail quietly later.


**The third answer, and how old the reading is** (`readAge`, `data-acctsay`) - sent as a screenshot of a Joint Checking row: *"this is a bit extreme, I need it to at least give me the option to use the current banking balance, not a figure from last month."*

The row offered two answers and **both were figures the app already held**: *Set it to this* accepts the ledger's expectation, *Bank still says $6,637.64* accepts a reading taken on 27 August. Neither is *"the bank says something else today"* - which is the only answer that can find money that moved without being logged, and therefore the only one the whole screen exists for. The balance input underneath could always take a real number; nothing said it was the way to answer the question, and it sat pre-filled with the stale figure, so the two chips read as the complete set of options.

A third chip now says **Neither - I will read it off the bank**, and it leads somewhere: the input is scrolled to, focused, selected and outlined, with a line saying what typing there will do. Nothing else changed about the mechanism - typing a balance already recorded a reading and computed the gap. What was missing was any indication that doing so was allowed.

And the reading's age is stated: *"Bank said this on 2026-08-27 - 6 days ago, last month"*, in red once it is a week old or from a previous month, unmarked when it is fresh. The date was always there and said nothing; a person should not have to do the subtraction themselves while two chips press them for an answer derived from it.


**A month that opens before payday** (`openingFor`, `monthToBudget`, `openingSuggestion`, `recDueThisMonth`, `renderRecFill`) - reported on the 1st: *"they told me to log my income but it didn't give me an opportunity to choose one of my already existing recurring payments... and it also didn't move any of my bank balance over that I already had accumulated from last month to cover these bills."* Two faults, both of them the app forgetting what it already knew.

**The money.** Zero-based budgeting funds the month from the money you **have**; this app was funding it only from money that arrived inside the calendar month. So somebody sitting on last month's wages, with rent due on the 3rd and payday on the 4th, opened a plan reading **minus two thousand dollars** and was told to log a paycheck that has not happened. This does **not** roll a leftover forward - that objection still stands, and inventing a carry-over number is exactly what the app refuses. It asks the person to **state** what was in the account when the month opened, the same act the accounts surface has always been built on, and then keeps that figure apart from income for the rest of its life: `monthToBudget` funds the plan, `monthIncome` measures earning, and the keep rate, the year projection and every earning statistic still read the second one. The offer appears in the note that explains the negative figure - where the person is already looking - defaults to *what is in the account now, less what has already been logged this month* so the two cannot double up, prints both halves whenever it is in play (*"Already in the account $2,600 + logged this month $0 = $2,600 to budget"*), and can be cleared in one tap.

**The paycheck.** The recurring rules held the name, the amount, the cadence, the account and the day - everything the log form was asking for - and the form had never heard of them. So the app nagged somebody to type in a paycheck it could already describe. Switching the form to **Income** now offers each occurrence of a recurring income rule that has **not yet landed**, dated the day it was due, with the working stated (*"Nothing is logged until you press the button below"*). Tapping fills the form; logging stamps the entry with its `recId`, so the calendar ticks itself, the ledger and the rule agree, and that payday can never be offered - or logged - twice. Only unlanded occurrences appear, and a biweekly rule correctly offers both of its paydays.


**A trail has to leave where you were standing** (`trailCloseSheets`, `goTrail(k, opts)`) - reported from a phone, from inside a category's own sheet: *"log against it pulls up the number pad but doesn't record anything."* It did exactly what it was told - switch to Track, focus the amount field - and it never closed the **sheet sitting on top of Track**. So the field took focus behind an overlay, the phone raised its number pad for a box nobody could see, and every keystroke went somewhere invisible. Every one of these breadcrumb buttons can be pressed from inside a sheet, so the close belongs in `goTrail` rather than at any one call site.

The second half, which the same tap exposed: walking to the log form **from a category** means logging against that category, and the trail did not carry one. Arriving with the picker on whatever happened to be selected is how a Sugarcreek expense gets filed under Rent - and the person has no reason to check, because they came from the right place. `trailBtn` takes an optional category now, and the two buttons inside a category's sheet pass theirs.


**Two from one intake, on the spending path** (`askHours`, `renderPacksStep` with `iaAns.packPick`, `removeCats`, cull mode) - both reported from watching a real person set the app up.

*"She chose bi weekly but it didn't ask her how many hours she worked."* It did not. Only the **hourly** branch of the pay-cadence helper ever asked - every other cadence called `finish(f, amt, 0)` and moved on, because the monthly figure does not need the hours. Nothing looked broken. What needs them is every *hours of your life* figure in the app, and with none on file `effectiveHourly()` falls back to a standard 2,080-hour year: the app **quietly assumed she works 40 hours a week**, which its own copy elsewhere calls the one thing it should never do. The hours are asked on every cadence now - **required** where the arithmetic depends on them (hourly), **skippable** everywhere else, with the reason stated (*"I do not need it for the budget, I need it to price things in hours of your life"*) and the cost of declining said out loud (*"if I guess I will guess 40"*).

*"There was no drop down that told her what was in each category. She chose all of the categories then found herself deleting everything inside of it and only keeping one option."* Exactly what the screen invited: a chip said **Getting around** and a counter said how many were behind it, and the contents stayed invisible until they were already on her Plan. `addPack(k, pick)` has **always** taken a per-category pick list - the intake step simply never passed one, the same shape as the growth-tag gap above. A pack now opens on tap, listing every category with the line somebody wrote to explain it, each one a tick. Opening still takes the whole pack, so one tap remains the fast path; the difference is that you can see what you are agreeing to before you agree. Emptying a pack by hand is the same statement as not taking it, the chip shows `4/6` when partial, and the button counts **categories, not packs**.

And for the plans that already got flooded - which is every install that ran the old step - **Remove several** on Plan turns the rows into ticks and removes them in one pass. It is the same delete, literally the same function: `removeCats` was extracted from the sheet's handler so a bulk remove could not grow its own copy of *"and their subtrees, and their assignments, and leave the transactions alone"*. The bar counts the subcategories that ride along rather than only what was ticked, and warns how many logged entries will survive as Uncategorized, because deleting a category never deletes money. Extracting it also closed a hole the single delete had all along: a **repeat rule pointing at a deleted category** was left behind, posting Uncategorized entries every month forever with nothing on screen to stop it.


**Saying that money is not spending** (`growthKindFor`, `growthRecType`, the *What kind of money is this* sheet section) - asked from a phone: *"last month I invested 1300... how do I plan for something like that, is it counted as an expense? Adding it to my income doesn't make sense either. Should I have an Acorns expense category, and if so it feels like a negative thing."*

It felt negative because it was being called negative. The **growth lane** has known since it shipped that investing is not spending - a tagged category posts as an `invest`, reaches net worth through *Invested capital*, and is kept out of the spending breakdown and the report. The gap was that the tag could only arrive by **accident**: picking a name out of a category pack that happened to carry one, or having typed a word like *"investing"* during the intake. There was no control anywhere that let somebody say it. Type *Acorns* into New category and you got a purchase, with nothing on screen offering an alternative.

Three fixes, and the last two are bugs the question uncovered rather than the feature itself.

**Say it.** The category sheet gains a *What kind of money is this* section: **Spending / Invested / Saved / Debt paid down**, four buttons rather than a checkbox because "not spending" is three different claims that read differently everywhere else in the app. Changeable at any time, on any category, and the note underneath states exactly what the tag does.

**Guess it, out loud.** `growthKindFor` knew the *words* for putting money away and none of the **names people actually type** - and nobody types "investment", they type the name of the app the money went into. It now reads `PUT_AWAY_WORDS` (extended with acorns, wealthfront, betterment, robinhood, wealthsimple, questrade, webull, coinbase, e\*trade, tfsa, rrsp, dividend, treasury and the rest) and splits it three ways, with **invest winning over save** when a name reads as both - *"Retirement savings"* is investing. A name it cannot place is left untagged, because a wrong tag here would tell somebody their groceries were building wealth. The add form now consults it, says it guessed, and points at the sheet.

**Two bugs behind the question.** Ticking *repeat* on a plan row hard-coded `type:'expense'`, so a monthly Acorns contribution would post as a purchase every month, flatly contradicting its own tag - the rule now takes its type from the category (`growthRecType`; `save` and `debt` stay expenses on purpose, since only `invest` has an asset to create behind it). And unticking matched rules by `type==='expense'`, so a growth rule survived the box being cleared and kept posting. Worse: `postRecurring`'s invest branch **never carried `catId`** - the lane grew up on Track where an invest has a source and no category - so a $1,300 monthly contribution showed *$1,300 assigned, $0 used*, every month, forever. The row the user was trying to fund read as never funded. `catUsed` already adds invested to spent; it just had nothing to add.


**The month you are in, and the month you walked to** (`monthAway`, `backToThisMonthHTML`, `#txMonthNote`, the boot snap-back) - reported from a phone: *"it's August 31st, why did it pull up September's tracking prematurely?"* Nothing was premature, and nothing had rolled over. `state.activeMonth` is **global** - Plan, Track and Reflect all read it - so one tap of the planning calendar's forward arrow, the control added so somebody could *plan ahead for future months*, relocated the entire app. And `activeMonth` is **saved**, so closing the tab and coming back the next day still landed in September.

Track was the worst of it, because Track has **no month control of any kind**. It rendered an empty next month - *"Income $0, Spent $0"* - with nothing on screen naming the month and no way back. Meanwhile the Plan label printed `<small>This month</small>` when the month matched and `<small>&nbsp;</small>` when it did not: **the absence of a word was carrying the whole message**, which is the silent state this app refuses everywhere else. An empty September reads as *"you have logged nothing"*, and that is a very different sentence from *"you are looking at next month"*.

Two rules came out of it. **Looking ahead is something you do inside a session, never the state you are greeted with** - so `boot()` refuses to open in a month that has not happened and comes home. Only forward: a **past** month still holds real figures and somebody may genuinely be part way through reconciling one, so that is kept. And **wherever a figure belongs to a month other than this one, the screen says so and offers one tap back** - a real pill on Plan and Reflect where the blank used to be (*Planning ahead · back to August*), and a banner on Track that names the month and explains what an empty screen there actually means.


**Read a statement** (`SCAN_GROUPS`, `scanGroupOf`, `scanMerge`, `scanRead`, `renderScanOut`, `scanToQuickLog`, the `scanRead` report signal) - asked for in one message: *"instead of taking actual screenshots is a scan feature possible where I can open up my bank statement and quickly skim all the transactions. Even if it wasn't completely uploaded into the track portion (though would be nice if it could at least analyze my spending habits and notice where I'm spending too much, to report back in reflect)."*

The reader already existed. It took **one photo** and its only destination was the quick log, so reading a four-page statement meant four separate passes and the reward for all of it was a pile of rows to check. Somebody who just wanted to know where their money went had to do a data-entry job first. The second half of that sentence is the actual feature: **the answer, without the import.**

So this path is a **reading**, not an import. Shoot up to twelve pages, read them in one pass on the device (same Tesseract, same never-leaves-the-phone promise, same *"show me what it actually read"*), and get back what went out, grouped, with the drip counted. `scanMerge` deduplicates across pages - overlapping shots re-read the same rows, and without it the drip count would move with how carefully somebody photographed their statement, which is the worst kind of wrong number. A repeat **within** one page is a real repeat and survives; a repeat of what an earlier page already gave is the overlap and is dropped, with the count reported.

Grouping runs on its own vocabulary (`SCAN_GROUPS`), not on the user's categories, so a brand-new install can scan last month before a single budget line exists. Ordered, first match wins, specific above the generic word inside it. What it cannot place goes to **Not recognised** and the share sitting there is printed - *"37% of your money went somewhere I could not name"* is a fact about the reading's own reliability, and hiding it would make every other percentage look more certain than it is.

**The drip** is the payoff: many small charges to one place, which a statement physically cannot show you because it spreads them over four pages. *"Takeout came to $96.17 across 4 separate charges, $24.04 at a time."* Nobody adds those up by eye.

Two rules it exists to keep. **Money put away is not money that went out** - a transfer into savings is shown, marked *this money stayed yours*, and excluded from both the headline and every percentage, because a headline that counts it reports somebody's best month as their worst. And **a reading never merges with the ledger**: it writes no transaction, moves no net worth, and `Put these in Track` hands the rows to the **quick log** (via `qlSeed`), where they get the review every imported row gets. OCR output and typed-in truth carry different confidence and adding them together produces a number that claims more than either half supports - the same fault as *A partial number wearing a whole number's name*, one commit earlier.

In Reflect the `scanRead` signal reports the last reading beside the ledger's own cards, never inside them: it names itself a reading, prints the pages and the date, names the biggest bucket with its working, and when it could not place 35%+ of the money it says *"treat this one gently"* before anything else. Kept readings are summaries - the last three, no merchant list warehoused in localStorage. Keys: `state.scans`.

*A footnote worth keeping: the first render filed **NETFLIX.COM** under retirement saving, because bare `includes('etf')` matches n-**etf**-lix. Matching is on word boundaries now (`scanMatch`), with multi-word keywords still matched as phrases. Three-letter tickers sitting in a list beside real merchant names make that failure the default, not the exception - and a subscription reported as savings moves money out of the column the app is trying to grow, silently.*


**A partial number wearing a whole number's name** (`ivCard`, `.iv-work`, `.iv-scope`) - sent from a phone as a photo of the invest panel with four words: *"This doesn't look right."* Both cards read as a minus - **-$17,809** one side, **-$14,452** the other - and both were captioned **net worth in 5 yrs**. The gap between them was exact to the cent, so the arithmetic was never the fault. `investCompare` only ever knew **the debts listed on that screen and the money aimed at them**: it returns `invest - debtLeft`, and it has no bank balance, no accounts, and no house behind the HELOC that was driving the figure. So it took a person with a home and savings and printed a number that said they were worth less than nothing.

Two ways to fix a wrong label, and only one of them is honest. Folding assets in would produce a fake five-year net worth - the model would be freezing a house and a chequing account for five years while modelling nothing else in the life around them. So the **figure stays exactly what it is** and stops claiming to be something else: the caption is *"left of this one decision after 5 yrs"*, each card prints the **two halves that made it** (`$0 invested` / `$17,809 still owed`) so a minus sign explains itself instead of accusing someone, and a scope line names what is **not** in there - the bank balance, and *your home behind Heloc* for any debt `debtSecuredOn` can see. It closes by pointing at the thing the decision actually hangs on: **the gap between the two cards**, which is untouched by every dollar left out, because those sit on both sides equally. Where a net worth is genuinely known it is quoted there in full, so the two numbers can be told apart on sight.

The rule this leaves behind: **a partial number may never wear a whole number's name.** A caption is an assertion, and this one asserted something the function had no way to know.

**Nobody knows the expected market return** (`RET_PRESETS`, `renderReturnPicker`, `OUTSIDE.market`, the `tooClose` guard) - the invest-vs-payoff comparison asked for an "expected market return %/yr" with an empty box, no anchor, and no hint that the number is a **guess about the future** rather than something the app knows. A 21-year-old has no way to answer it, and neither does anyone else. Three fixes: figures to **pick** rather than type (5% cautious, 7% historical after inflation, 10% historical before inflation); a note saying plainly that nobody knows, anchoring it in roughly a century of US market history (about 10%/yr before inflation, about 7% after) and adding that this is **history, not a promise**, since any single decade can look nothing like it; and a line that answers the real question - **does the guess even change the answer?** - by running the same comparison at 5% and at 10% and saying whether the winner flips. Where it does not flip, the user does not need to predict anything. The same check drives a `tooClose` guard on the verdict: a $96 gap on $237,000 after ten years, or any result that reverses across the plausible range, is a **tie** and neither card is highlighted as the winner - crowning one told people a decision had been made when the honest answer was that it had not. A genuinely lopsided case (a 29.9% card) still gets its clear verdict.

**A gate opens when its data arrives** (`applyPanelGates` hooked to `save`) - reported from a real phone: two debts entered, and the "Your way out" panel still said *"Add a debt above."* `applyPanelGates()` ran in exactly two places, `boot()` and `renderAll()`, and adding a debt calls `save()` and `renderDebt()` - neither of which is either. So the panel kept the `panel-waiting` class it was handed at boot, which **hides its own controls**, including the monthly-amount field that makes the screen work. It cleared on a reload, which is why it survived testing. **All five gates had it**, not only this one: the CSV importer, the circulation ratio, the vault, the payoff planner and the invest comparison were every one stale until something happened to trigger a full render. The check is hooked to `save()` now - the single place every state change passes through - so no future feature can forget it the way this one was forgotten. The cost is a handful of `.length` reads beside a full `JSON.stringify` of state, which is noise against what `save()` already does.

**The import that ate everything** (`handleImportFile`, `looksEncrypted`, `looksLikeAppData`, `applyImported`, `undoImport`) - reported from a real phone: took an encrypted backup, pressed **Import**, and the app started over. The plain importer accepted anything that parsed as JSON - and an encrypted `.acct` envelope **is** valid JSON, carrying `{app,v,alg,kdf,iter,salt,iv,data}` and not one state key. So `Object.assign(defaultState(), envelope)` produced a pristine empty app, **saved it over the real data**, rebooted into onboarding, and said `"Imported."` Silent, total, irreversible loss reported as success, on the one path whose entire job is preventing loss. Any JSON did it - a `package.json`, an empty object, a file from another app. Three things were missing: **know what the file is** (an envelope now goes to the decrypter and asks for the passphrase), **refuse what you do not recognise** rather than importing it (refusing costs nothing; a wrong import costs everything), and **a way back** (the previous state is stashed before any replace, and Settings shows *Undo the import* while a stash exists). Every restore path - the Import button, the Restore button, the encrypted decrypter - now goes through one guarded door that names what is here and what is in the file before replacing anything. Pinned in `tests/structure.mjs` section 26, which reports `cats 0 / tx 0 / onboarded false` against the old importer.

**You pick a voice, not an age** (the intake `gate` step, `renderRegister`) - the **first question in the app** used to ask an age bracket - `Gen Z · under 30`, `Middle · 30-55`, `Mature · 55+` - and then used the answer to choose the **slang**. So a 23-year-old who does not say "bestie" picked "under 30" because it was true about them, and got talked to in a voice they do not use. The question asked about identity and the answer controlled vocabulary; those are not the same thing, and sorting people's words by their birthday is a stereotype with a database behind it. It asks how the app should talk now, and shows a **real line from each voice** so the choice is made by ear: `Loose` / `Plain` / `Measured`. Settings matches, dropping "the generation dial" for a live sample pulled from the actual `TRAP_RESPONSES` matrix at the intensity currently set - a sample written specially for the picker would be a lie about what you are choosing. The internal keys (`genz`, `middle`, `mature`) are untouched, so every copy matrix in the file still works; only what the human sees changed.

**Turn off what you do not want** (`HIDEABLE`, `SPINE`, `areaOff`, `applyAreaVisibility`) - the stage ladder decides what someone is **ready** for; this decides what they **want**, which is a different question only they can answer. Six areas can be switched off in Settings (Shield, Debt, Reflect, Build, Learn, Diary), leaving a clean Home/Plan/Track dashboard for someone who wants exactly that. The spine - Home, Plan, Track, Settings - cannot be hidden: switching off Settings would bury the switch that turns things back on, and hiding Plan or Track leaves an app with nothing to do; `normalizeState` filters `areasOff` down to the hideable six so a corrupt or hand-edited save cannot do it either. Hiding is a **view** choice and never a delete: every figure and entry stays, and comes back untouched. The hard part is that **everything able to navigate has to agree** - the tab bar, `activateTab` (which redirects a hidden area to Home, so no shortcut or stale history entry can land there), the app map (which also recounts "N of M areas used" against what it shows), and the "I want to…" hub. The hub needed an explicit `area` tag rather than inference: the gut-check opens a **modal** instead of navigating, so with Shield off the hub kept offering the single feature that person had just said they did not want.

**The time ledger reaches the report** (`timeWeek` signal) - "Your week in hours" was **write-only**. You tapped `+1 hr`, a caption underneath updated, and `state.timeLog` was never read by anything else in the app: not Reflect, not the report, not Home. Hours are the app's own currency, so a logged week now lands in the Accountability Report beside the money, on the same rails as every other signal: silent below five logged hours, the split shown as its working (`27 logged = 21 invested + 6 leaked`), and the leaked hours priced at the user's own rate, which is the only thing that makes the number mean anything.

**Outside numbers** (`OUTSIDE`, `outsideFresh`, the `o*` report signals) - inflation and rates, **shipped, not fetched**. The app promises "nothing leaves your device", so it does not call anyone to ask what CPI is: the figures are baked at build time and refreshed on deploy (`docs/DEPLOY.md` has the step). Four cards ride the report's rails, each tied to the user's own budget and never shown as trivia: prices up 3.4% against **their** hourly rate (with "has not changed since March" when the app watched the wage get set - `state.wageSetAt`); grocery inflation against **their** food line ("a year ago about $633 bought the same cart"); the national average savings APY against **their** idle cash, average and high-yield both priced in dollars a year with no account named; and the average new-card APR against **their** dearest debt. Every card is stamped with its as-of date on screen and labelled "baked into the app, never fetched"; a standing line under them says the numbers say what is, not what to do. The CPI card requires a wage the user actually set - `effectiveHourly()`'s income-based estimate is not a footing for "your hour buys 3% less". And a build whose figures have aged past 240 days **goes quiet on its own**, saying the numbers are too old to show as current - stale-but-silent is the designed failure mode, because a stale figure presented as current is the one way this feature could hurt someone. Equities were considered and dropped on purpose: a daily-moving number beside the impulse tools invites exactly the behaviour the app exists to fight.

**The Accountability Report** (`REPORT_SIGNALS`, `buildReport`, `renderReport`) - the app measured plenty and concluded nothing. It knew the top category, the priciest weekday, net worth, income against expense, and it never once put two months side by side and said what changed; the user held two screens in their head and did the comparing, which is the one job software is better at than people. Reflect now **opens on the verdict**, with the charts underneath as the evidence. Nine signals: on-pace for the month while there is still time to act, what you kept (with the three-month trend), **the year** (below), the biggest category drift against last month, a habit that **started**, a habit that **stopped**, the essentials' share of income, investing consistency across three months, and the hours of life the month cost. Four rules, each inherited rather than invented: **silence over speculation** (every signal declares the data it needs and does not appear below it - `spendTendencies()` has always worked this way; what is missing is listed separately as something to unlock); **show the working** (every card carries the arithmetic that produced it, because a conclusion nobody can check is one nobody should trust); **observation then a choice** (the nudge is always a fact about their own numbers - "at this rate that is about $1,440 a year" - never an instruction); and **never congratulate someone who is drowning** (a month that went negative leads with the bad news, and a keep rate refuses to render at all when nothing has been spent, because "you kept 100%" after one paycheck measures what has not been logged rather than what was kept). A signal that throws is dropped rather than taking the page with it.

**A posted bill says which bill** (`recTxNote`, `normalizeState` backfill) - every entry a recurring schedule posted was noted `Recurring`, so four bills in a row read `Recurring, Recurring, Recurring, Recurring` and the list said nothing at all. The transaction already carries `recId`, so the app knows perfectly well that it was automatic; the note is the one place that should say **what it was**. It names the bill now - the category for an expense, the source for a paycheck - and "posted automatically" moves to the sub line as `· repeats`, which has room for it. `normalizeState` backfills entries already saved under the old note, from the schedule that made them or from the category they landed in when that schedule is gone, so past months read properly without anyone re-logging anything. It only ever rewrites the exact placeholder, so a note somebody typed themselves (`"Recurring bill I typed myself"`) is never touched.

**The multi-line log guesses the category** (`suggestCatFor`, `HABIT_WORDS`, `qlCatOptions`) - every row's dropdown defaulted to whichever category happened to be **first**, so a notepad full of coffees logged itself as `Rent / mortgage` unless you corrected every line by hand. A wrong confident default is worse than none, because it looks decided. Rows start on **Which category?** and the app earns the answer instead, strongest evidence first: (1) the user's own past entries with the same description - a habit they repeat, which is the whole point; (2) a bill they already told the app **repeats**, matched by name through `state.recurring`; (3) the category and subcategory names themselves; (4) common purchase vocabulary, pointed **only** at categories that already exist, so it never invents one. A guess lands on a subcategory over its parent, because the sub is the more specific home. Below a confidence floor it guesses **nothing** rather than guessing wrong. Guesses are **marked** (accent left border) so they never read as decisions, and it only ever fills a dropdown nobody has touched - one correction is permanent and no amount of further typing overwrites it. The OCR path calls the same function, which is where it pays off most: a photographed notepad arrives categorised. Whatever it could not guess logs as **Uncategorized** and the toast says how many, rather than filing it under something wrong and silent.

**A mode you can leave from where you are** (`setReorder`, `.reorder-done`) - Reorder is a mode, and the only way out of it sat in a toolbar at the top of the list. Move something to the bottom of a long plan and you had to scroll all the way back up to say you were finished. A **Done reordering** pill now floats above the bar for as long as the mode is on (the FAB hides while it does - one clear action at a time), and finishing scrolls you back to the toolbar so the next thing you reach for is where you left it. The toolbar button itself had turned into an empty outlined pill the moment you pressed it: it carries `.btn ghost primary`, and `.ghost` is declared **after** `.primary` at the same specificity, so `.ghost` won the background while `.primary` still set the text to `--on-accent` - near-white letters on a near-white panel. `.btn.ghost.primary` now names the combination, which settles it for every such button rather than patching the one that was noticed. `tests/palette.mjs` could never have caught this: the fault is not in any token pair, it is in which rule won on one live element.

**The year nobody works out for themselves** (`year` signal) - people know what lands on Friday; almost nobody knows the twelve-month total, and the gap between *"I make $840 a week"* and *"I handle $43,680 a year"* is the difference between feeling broke and knowing what you are working with. The signal projects from **what actually landed** once there are two full months of logged income, and from what they told setup before that - and it always says which of the two it is doing, because a projection that hides its source is just a confident number. Three guards, each of which would otherwise produce a lie with a decimal point on it: the **month in progress is never annualised** (four days of income is not a salary), a **single logged month is never annualised** (one commission month becomes a career), and where the logged months differ by more than 20% the nudge reports the year as a **range rather than a promise**. It prices the year in hours of their life, and where the cadence is known it names it - 26 paydays make a biweekly year, and two of those months carry an extra one.

**The one number the app prices against, out of hiding** (`renderTrueRateCard`, `trueRateDue`, `applyTrueRate`) - every hours-of-life figure in the app depends on the true hourly rate, and the calculator that sets it sat inside a **collapsed `<details>` in Settings**: two taps and a scroll behind a summary nobody opens. Which is the same as not existing. It now meets people **on Home** the moment setup ends, with their own take-home already in the field, asking the two questions that separate a paycheck from a rate: the commute and prep you are not paid for, and what the job costs you in gas, lunches and clothes. It shows the working before you fill it in, names what those were costing per hour once you do (*"the commute and the overhead were costing you $4.37 an hour and nobody was billing you for it"*), and then **leaves for good** - used or waved off, `state.trueRateSkipped` makes the dismissal stick, because a card that comes back is nagging rather than offering. Settings keeps its copy for anyone redoing it, and both doors run `applyTrueRate` so a rate can never differ by where you typed it.

**The only scoreboard the app cannot flatter itself with** (`bankTrend` signal, `daysSinceBalance`) - the framing, in the founder's words: *this is not really a budgeting app, it is an accountability app - and since it never pulls bank data, whatever is actually in their accounts is the reflection of how well it works.* That is structurally true and the app now says it. **Every other figure here is built from what somebody chose to type**: log less and the app looks calmer, log nothing and it has nothing bad to say - the standing weakness of a tool with no bank connection. The account balance is the exception **precisely because the app cannot reach it**: typed in freely, from outside, against no incentive to be generous. So its movement over months is the one honest answer to *"is any of this working"* - the budget is the process, the balance is the outcome. Which is exactly why it is reported carefully: a rising balance is **not proof the app worked** (a raise, a refund and an unpaid bill look identical from that height), a fall is not called a failure, and level is called level. It says what moved, says what it cannot know, and stops. Two readings minimum - one is not a trend, and the locked line carries a trail to the place that fixes it. The panel where the number lives says why it is different from all the others, and a balance not touched in ten days is named as a **fact rather than a nag** - shown late, gone the moment it is current.

**Trends showed one thing and called itself Trends** (`TREND_SERIES`, `renderTrendSeries`, `metricSnapshot`) - three asks in one note. **First**, *"where does that $13,000 figure come from"* sitting under a net worth of $4,843: it is neither net worth nor the bank, it is everything the **ledger** has net-added since the first entry - a record of your typing, not of your wealth - and it was labelled "Latest balance" directly beneath a number it is supposed to differ from. It says what it is now (*"Logged in minus logged out, all time"*) and the `?` reconciles all three, including the most common reason the ledger runs high: **income logged twice**, once by hand and once by a recurring rule. **Second**, Trends drew one month of spending categories. It now carries five series - Spending, Investing (cumulative, because that is what growing means), In the bank, Goals and Net worth - with the difference between them stated on screen rather than hidden: spending and investing are **recomputed from the ledger** so they reach as far back as the entries do, while bank, goals and net worth have **no history of their own** and can only come from the monthly snapshot. **Third**, that snapshot now carries `bank` and `goalTarget`, and it is re-taken whenever an account is added, edited or removed - editing a balance overwrites the number and the old one is gone forever, so the snapshot is the only place a balance can ever leave a trace, and taking it only at boot meant today's change did not reach the trend until tomorrow. A month with no snapshot draws a **gap, not a zero**, and a single snapshot refuses to draw a line at all: *"this line needs a second month before it can be a line."*

**The app could not record being paid** (`INCOME_SOURCES.yield`, `a.pays`, `l.apr`) - asked directly: how are HELOCs and cards viewed in Build, why is there no area for dividends or interest, and is the philosophy to forbid leverage? Three answers, all of them **gaps rather than positions**. The app teaches that an asset is the thing that *"puts money IN your pocket"* and that the top rung of the Growth Ladder is *"own things that pay you"* - then offered five income types, **every one of them a form of labour**, so the independent-income ladder that celebrates escaping wage work could only ever be fed by more work. `yield` and `rent` join it, count as independent income (the purest kind), and carry no hours, so they never touch the true hourly rate. An asset's only recurring field was what it **drains**; it can now say what it **pays**, with the net stated per year (*"this one genuinely pays you"*), and the panel shows both sides instead of only the cost of keeping things. And a liability was a name and a number - a 3% mortgage and a 29% card were the same object - so *"import from my liabilities"* pushed every one into the payoff planner at **apr:0**, after which the planner warned that its own figures were understated. Liabilities carry a rate now and it travels. **On leverage the app takes no position and never did**: rung 3 says *"kill the interest - debt is a negative return you're guaranteed to pay"*, which is about rate, not about borrowing. What it could not do was tell the two apart.

**A door to every room that still has something in it** (`unlockReachableTabs`) - from a phone, having reached the Accounts panel: *"where does this page live? I was only able to find it because of this"* - pointing at a link on Reflect's net-worth view. Accounts lives on **Build**, and it is deliberately **ungated**: "what's in the bank" is a fact, not a wealth-stage tool, and Home already shows spend-mode users their bank total. But the Build **button** carries `data-stage="2"`, and spending mode locks every `[data-stage]` element - so the tab vanished and took the one ungated panel inside it along with it. The only way in was that single link on another tab, which deep-linked into a **room with no door**: reachable once, never findable again. The rule is now about the room rather than the label on it - a tab is hidden only when everything inside it is hidden, and symmetric, so a tab whose contents all close later closes too rather than becoming a door to an empty room. `activateTab` also refuses to land on a locked tab, the same guard `goTrail` already had for switched-off areas, so a future gate cannot recreate the dead link. And the link that was the only way in now promises only what it can deliver: *"Edit my accounts"* in spending mode, *"Edit what you own and owe"* when the rest of Build is actually open.

**The report had no voice** (`RP_TONE`, `rpSay`) - from a phone: *"These words have lost its savage."* They had. The Accountability Report - the verdict layer, the part that tells you what your month means - had **14 signals and zero voice calls**. Everyone read the same flat prose whether they had asked for clean, blunt or savage, on the app's flagship surface. The arithmetic is deliberately untouched: `body` and `work` are facts and facts have no tone. What gained a voice is the **closer**, which was always an opinion. Two rules that pull opposite ways: signals judging **their spending** pass a context and get floored to clean on anything sensitive, while an **outside number is not a judgement of them** and passes `ctx:null` on purpose - the app keeps its teeth about the world without turning them on the person reading it. Deterministic rather than random, because a report that reworded itself on every visit would feel unstable. The wider sweep is in `docs/Voice-Audit.md`: **21 of 24 surfaces reach no voice engine at all**, of which about twelve should - and the rest (the tripwire disclosure, the vault timers, the charts, the forms, the user's own diary) are correctly flat, because voice on a factual disclosure undermines it.

**A rule that had not started cannot be broken** (`calIsPre`) - from a phone: *"if the days prior to August 23rd are greyed out, am I to assume the red 1 is for September? The logic is wrong."* It was **August 1**, scarlet and alone in a wall of grey. `calIsPre` made an exception for days that had spending on them, so the reward calendar gave **no credit** for anything before the start date and handed out **every penalty** - the sentence above the grid said earlier days were "not counted" while counting that one, the streak read **0**, and the month read **$1,842 over pace**, on a day whose entries were mostly the app's own seeded recurring posts. Asymmetric scoring is not accountability, it is retroactive blame. A day before you started is now not scored at all, green or red. The money is untouched everywhere it was real - Track, the month totals, every figure in Reflect - it simply is not marked against a rule that had not begun. Nothing is hidden either: a pre-start day with money on it keeps a **dot**, stays tappable, and says what it is (*"it counts in your ledger and every total on Reflect, and it is not scored here, because there was no allowance to beat yet"*) rather than scolding you. The sentence above the grid now describes what the grid actually does, and counts the dotted days out loud. And the calendar gained its own **month arrows** - it had always shown `state.activeMonth` with no way to move it, so the only month control lived on Plan and Reflect, tabs away from the screen people actually live on.

**The date beside the switch** (`repeatCtrlHTML`, `data-repdate`) - ticking the repeat toggle on a category offered a frequency and nothing else, and *"monthly"* with no date is the app picking one. It picked the **1st**, hard-coded, and the only place to correct it was the Recurring panel at the very bottom of the tab - past every category, which nobody scrolls to just to move a bill from the 26th to the 1st. The date now sits next to the switch that raised the question, appears only once the thing repeats, and goes away with it. Two things changed underneath: the new anchor is **the 1st that has not happened yet** rather than this month's, because anchoring to a date already gone back-posts a bill - and if the person logged that rent by hand, it lands twice; and a blank date is ignored rather than treated as an instruction. The layout suite failed the moment the control landed (a native date field has a floor width of `mm/dd/yyyy`, which pushed the Plan row 34px past a 320px screen), so on a phone the repeat controls take their own line.

**Money you have not been paid is not income** (`payDay` step, `postRecurring` in `commitIntake`) - from a phone: *"money should not be assumed either if it hasn't been accounted for. Say I don't get paid til the 29th - it shouldn't be income not yet received if it's only the 26th."* Correct, and the app already agreed with itself **everywhere except one place**. `postRecurring` caps the current month at today on purpose - *"spent has to be a fact, not a forecast"* - so a paycheck due on the 29th does not post on the 26th. But `commitIntake` wrote a paycheck dated the **1st** whether or not the person had been paid, and every figure built on it inherited that: left to budget, the net tile, the glance panel. One date settles it, so it gets asked: **"when did you last actually get paid?"** - the *last* payday rather than the next, because that is a fact people know precisely. The recurring rule is anchored there at the cadence the income step already captured (weekly and biweekly carrying the **per-payday** amount), and `commitIntake` now writes **no transaction at all** - it calls `postRecurring(M)`, which posts exactly what has come due by today. A monthly paycheck due on the 29th posts nothing on the 25th; one that landed on the 9th posts **on the 9th**, not today and not the 1st; a biweekly anchor four weeks back correctly lands two paydays. Optional, because a new job has no answer yet and a made-up date is worse than none - skipping starts the rule next month. Two bugs fell out of building it: `why` had only ever been wired into the chip renderers, so `haveNow` declared a reason and rendered **no button**; and re-running setup dropped the intake's recurring rules while keeping the transactions they had posted, so the next run **doubled the month's income**. `tests/intake_cost.mjs` caught the new question the moment it landed and forced both the advertised counts (16→17, 23→24) and the sentence the app says out loud to move with it, which is exactly what that suite is for.

**Totals that open** (`glanceFold`) - the glance panel showed *Money in $13,700* with nothing behind it, which is a number you have to take on faith. Each side now expands to the **last five that made it**, newest first, with a *"+ N more this month"* line so nothing is silently truncated. One side at a time, so the panel cannot outgrow the phone.

**The number nobody could trace** (`dupBalance`, `FIELD_WHY.txNet`) - asked after three days of use: *"where does the net in Balance come from?"* Two faults behind one question. The Track strip printed the **same arithmetic twice**: `Net` and `Balance (all time)` are both income minus spending minus investing, differing only by window - and on the all-months view they are identical **by construction**, while for anyone with a single month of history they coincide on the month view too. Two adjacent tiles, both saying "net", same figure. It now appears only when it says something the visible-window net does not. And nothing explained that most of the income side was written by the **setup chat**: it dates a paycheck to the 1st because the person told it their monthly take-home, which is a reasonable thing to do and a terrible thing to leave unexplained to someone on day three, whose $3,185 is a $3,200 paycheck that may not have landed yet minus $15 of real coffee. The `?` on the tile now shows the arithmetic, names the seeded portion and its share, says plainly that *"if that money has not actually arrived yet, this reads high"*, points at the rows in the log below, and says to delete or correct them - *"the app will not argue"* - while still separating logged money from bank money.

**Not everything is a trap** (`TRAPS.tool`, `toolPayback`, `renderToolResult`) - a phone screenshot: someone scanned a **$100 leaf mulcher**. Every autumn they had fought the leaves with a rake that could not do it, the alternative was paying somebody, and the tool solved it. The app called it a **Scroll Trap** and told them *"Meta ran the numbers and bet you'd cave. Prove them wrong."* That is not a gut-check, it is an accusation - and an app that calls every purchase a trap is worth exactly as much as one that calls none of them a trap, because neither is telling you anything. The Trap Radar had four lanes and all four assumed you had been got. There was a not-a-trap path already, but it was for **events** (the car broke), reached from the talk-through - a considered, cost-replacing purchase had nowhere to go. So there is a fifth answer: *"none of these - it replaces something I already pay for, in money or in hours."* It is **not a permission slip**: it asks what the thing displaces (amount and hours, each with its own period picker) and does the payback arithmetic. The verdict card becomes a payback card - what it displaces a year, how long until it has paid for itself, year-one and five-year net - the needle points where the arithmetic points rather than always at Trap, the shaming voice line is gone, and the buy button stops calling it a ransom. Answer "nothing" and it says so plainly: *"that is an honest answer, and it means this is a want - which is allowed. But it will not be filed as an investment, because calling it one is how people talk themselves into things."* A slow payback is called slow. **The double-count is the part that matters**: giving both a hired cost and the hours it saves would let the app add them, and you would have done **one** of them, not both - so it takes the larger, names the one it set aside, and says why (*"adding them together would flatter the number, which is the trick this app exists to catch"*). Two caveats are non-optional on every payback: it only pays back if you actually use it, and the cost it replaces has to be one you would genuinely have paid.

**Money that did not leave** (`__invest` / `__income` in `qlCatOptions`, `PUT_AWAY_WORDS`, `putAwayLine`) - from a phone: *"the track category still assumes expense even though investing should be an option. Say I used the $145 towards savings or investment, there's really no indication - it just has a big expense column. Its connotations are destructive not rewarding."* Both halves were true. **Your order, not creation order** (`catOrder`, `moveCat`, `catReorder`, `category.sort`) - category order was array order, which is creation order, which is an accident. People read a budget in a shape that means something to them: the rent at the top because it frightens them, or the fun money at the top because it is what they overspend. **Reorder** on the Plan toolbar turns on a mode that puts a `⠿` grip on every row, and you drag it where you want it. It started as `↑`/`↓` buttons on the theory that HTML5 drag does not fire on touch - true, but the conclusion was wrong: **pointer events** carry touch, mouse and stylus down one path with no library, and `touch-action:none` on the grip alone is what stops the browser claiming the gesture as a page scroll. That is why the handle is a handle and not the whole row - the list still has to scroll normally under a finger that is not on the grip. Arrows also meant counting taps to move a category down eleven rows, with the list re-rendering under your thumb after each one. Rows are **not** shuffled in the DOM mid-drag: the source stays put and greys out, a ghost follows the pointer and a line shows where it will land, and only the release writes anything (`moveCatTo`, then one `renderBudget()`). Shuffling live is where the bugs are, because the plan is a flat list holding three nesting levels plus split notes and add-rows, so "move this element" means "move it and the four unrelated-looking ones that belong to it". A drag is confined to the row's own siblings, so a subcategory can never be dropped into the top level however far you drag it. Auto-scroll runs while a finger sits near the edge of the glass, or you could only ever drop something already on screen. **Escape** abandons a drag. The grip is still a real `<button>`, so **arrow keys move a row too** - drag is the preferred way, not the only way, and a screen reader keeps a path. Subcategories reorder **inside** their parent and can never escape it. While the mode is on the editing controls **collapse** rather than dim: dimming still costs the width, and on a subcategory row the arrows plus a greyed-out amount squeezed the name to `"E..."`. Order is an explicit `sort` field rather than array position, so it survives merges, imports and a restore from backup, all of which rebuild the array; `normalizeState` seeds it from current position, so an existing budget keeps exactly the order it had. **`categoryOptions()` routes through `topCats`/`childrenOf`** - it used to filter `state.categories` raw, so reordering the plan left every category picker in creation order and the app disagreed with the user about their own arrangement.

**Let's talk it through** (`#talkSheet`, `openTalk`/`renderTalk`, `CAUSES`, `state.lessons`) - a conversational lane for money that left because something *happened*. The Trap Radar requires a trap before it will speak, and all four (scroll, friction, status, leak) assume temptation, so an accident has no honest answer there. **Charts you can interrogate** (`chartHits`, `chartRead`, `CHART_REDRAW`, `inOutRead`, `ledgerRead`, `trendRead`) - from a phone: *"these graphs mean actually nothing unless you're able to click on them and interact with them. Each point gets a full-height invisible hit band (a 44%-wide bar on a six-column phone chart is about 20px, under every touch-target minimum), the selection survives the redraw, and a readout underneath says what the point is **made of**: the month split into in, out and put away, the biggest entry on each side, the entries behind it, the move since the last recorded point, and for the running total what the line is *not* - not the bank balance, not net worth. **A line per category, and a door to the rest** (`rowFor` in `renderBudget`, `openCatSheet`, `renderCatSheet`, `wirePlan`) - from a phone: *"this scroll is long on the plan tab, it should adopt this layout, with a details tab holding the past history and recurring options and targets."* Correct. Eleven controls each, times twenty categories, and the one question a plan exists to answer - *what is left in this* - was buried in the middle of them.

The Plan list is now three columns and nothing else: **name, assigned, available**. Reported from a real phone with the system font turned up, twice, both times as *"not a fan of the text overflow"*: a category name sliced through the middle of a letter, and the Available column shortened to `$4...`. **The ledger is one line, and an entry can finally be changed** (`renderTxList`, `openTxSheet`, `renderTxSheet`, `editTx`, `deleteTx`, `syncInvestAsset`) - the same move as the Plan tab, applied to Track after the same request. A row carried a date, a category chip, a note, a second line with the energy tag and whether it repeats and which month it was in, a life-cost badge and a delete cross: two lines and six things, on a list meant to be scanned. The category rides along only where there is measurably room for it (a long note plus a long category needs about 500px of row, so it shows on a tablet and not on a phone), and below 340px the row goes to two lines like a bank statement rather than shaving a note to 27px.

The sheet closed a gap that had been open since the beginning: **there was no way to edit a transaction**, only to delete it - and deleting one takes the gut-check and the giving record attached to it along with the typo. Amount, date, category, source, income kind, invest kind and note are all editable, and so is the **energy tag**, which drove the Circulation chart and had been write-once since it was built.

Editing exposed a drift the old code could not avoid. `investAssetAdd` moves the auto-managed *Invested capital* asset by a **delta**, which is correct for adding and deleting and wrong for editing: if the asset is missing - deleted by hand on Build, or absent from a hand-edited backup - it gets created at the size of the correction rather than the size of the holding, and net worth is off by exactly the amount that was fixed. `syncInvestAsset()` recomputes it from the invest transactions that still hold value, so it cannot drift; it runs after every edit and delete, and once more in `normalizeState` so an imported backup heals itself. (It builds its own id rather than calling `uid()`, because `normalizeState` runs inside `load()` and `const uid` is not initialised until after `let state = load()` - reaching for it there threw, `load()` fell back to a default state, and every category vanished on boot.)

**The streak is about what you still choose** (`isScoredSpend`, `scoredSpentByDay`, `billsTotal`) - asked from a phone: *"is your spending streak the same as your recurring budget?"* It was, and it should not have been. Every expense counted against the daily allowance, auto-posted bills included, so a rent of $1,250 landing on the 13th put that day $1,200 over and reset the under-budget streak - every month, forever. Measured before the fix: twenty clean days ran a streak of **26**, and adding one posted rent cut it to **13**. A streak that resets on rent day is measuring the calendar, not the person.

A bill you already decided on is not a decision you make again on the day it lands. Anything the app posted from a recurring rule is now excluded from the daily allowance, the streak, the pace and the monthly limit bar - and **still shown**, on the day card and in every total, tagged *bill · not scored*, in exactly the way a pre-start day is shown and not scored. Hiding money is worse than mis-scoring it. The monthly limit bar also names the excluded total out loud (*"plus $1,250 of bills you set to repeat, which this limit does not count"*), so the two figures on that screen cannot silently disagree.

The seam is stated rather than hidden: **a bill typed in by hand still counts**, because nothing can tell it from a purchase. Setting it to repeat stops it counting, which is the behaviour worth encouraging anyway.

**A category you can make where you are standing** (`refreshCatSelects`, `resolveCatChoice`, `syncCatNew`) - asked from a phone, looking at a category dropdown: *"what happens if it's a different field? I'm not able to choose my own."* Nothing happened. Every picker listed the categories that already existed and offered no way to make one, so an expense fitting none of them meant leaving the form, crossing to Plan, adding a category, coming back and starting the entry again - and the likelier outcome is that the expense never gets logged. The quick-log rows had solved this already, with a **＋ New category…** option and a name field beside it; that affordance simply never reached the log form, the recurring form or the transaction sheet. All four carry it now, from one shared option list, and an unnamed one is refused rather than filed somewhere else - a transaction is never written against the literal `__new` choice.

**One list of ways money comes in** (`INCOME_SOURCES`, `incomeSourceOptions`, `incomeKindLabel`) - asked from a phone while logging a paycheck, looking at five options: *"shouldn't my options that I have in my plan be these?"* No - the Plan is where money **goes** and this asks where it **came from**, and the Type answers one question the app actually uses: is this from one employer, or from something you own or built (`isIndependent`, `isYieldSrc`). The name of the income is the free text field beside it.

But the question found a real fault. The log form carried its own hand-typed copy of the five original kinds, so when **Dividends / interest** and **Rent / royalties** were added to the model - specifically to answer a question about capital income - they appeared in the entry sheet and nowhere else. The one screen where people actually log a paycheck still offered five. Both selects are built from `INCOME_SOURCES` now, and the suite scans the **source file** (not the rendered DOM, where a generated option and a hand-typed one are indistinguishable) to assert no screen keeps a second copy to drift from.

It also gained **Something else**, for the same reason the category pickers did: a fixed list of five ways to earn money is a claim about the world, and it is wrong. Choosing it asks for a name, stored as `srcLabel`. It counts as income you built rather than a primary job, and is deliberately **not** treated as yield - if the money arrives without you, Dividends or Rent is the honest choice.

**The ledger and the bank, introduced** (`acctLoggedSince`, `acctExpected`, `bankExpected`, `defaultAcctId`, `txAcctChoice`) - asked directly: *"how do I change my bank balance to reflect the income I received?"* You could not. The two halves of the app were separate universes: the ledger knew what had been logged, the accounts knew what you last told them the bank said, and **no transaction had ever carried an account** - `accountId` appeared zero times in the codebase. Logging $715.97 of income left checking sitting at exactly what it was.

What it deliberately does **not** do is post straight to the balance. That would make the balance only as accurate as the typing, and the entire value of that number is that it comes from outside. So:

- Transactions carry `acctId`. The picker appears on the log form only when there is more than one account - one account is an answer, not a choice, so entries are filed against it silently. Bills the app posts itself carry it too, or the expectation would ignore exactly the entries written on your behalf.
- **expected = what the bank said, when you said it, plus everything logged against that account since.** The account row shows both figures and offers *Set it to this*.
- **Reconciling is where it earns its keep.** Type what the bank actually says, and the difference from the expectation is money that moved and never got logged - the one thing an app built on self-reporting can otherwise never see. It is captured (`lastGap`, `lastRecon`) and named in words on the row.
- Net worth and runway still run on the **stated** balance, not the expectation. The scorecard stays the scorecard.

**The one-time catch-up** (`#acctBackfill`, `backfillAcct`) - everything logged before transactions carried an account has none, so it counts toward nothing, and telling somebody to re-enter a year of history is not an answer. The Accounts panel offers to file the lot in one move, to a named account (with a picker when there is more than one). What makes it safe is that it **states its own effect before it happens**: it names exactly how far the expected balance will move and how many of the entries can move it at all - usually very few, because only entries dated after an account's balance date count, so filing a year of history typically moves the number by nothing. The test asserts the promise and the outcome match to the cent. It disappears once nothing is homeless, and is never offered when there is no account to file to.

Two edges that would go silently wrong and are pinned as tests: a balance typed **today** must not be double-counted by entries dated today (strictly-after, because transactions carry a date and not a time - said out loud rather than hidden), and accepting the app's own figure records **no gap**, because nothing came from outside and claiming one would be inventing evidence.

**The fast way in knows where the money came from** (`qlAcctRowHTML`, `qlAcctChoice`, `qlAskAccount`) - from a phone, on the quick log: *"instead of making this group list so long just have an add another feature to keep things tight - also it looks like it's missing a funding source to say which bank balance you want to add or withdraw from."* Both right. It opened on **three empty rows**, which reads as a form to fill rather than a line to jot, and it was the one path into the app that put **no account** on anything - so the fastest way to log was also the only way to manufacture the exact history the catch-up on Build then has to rescue.

It opens on one line now and grows from **＋ Add another line**. The account is asked **once for the batch**, above the lines, not once per line: a notepad of purchases came off a single card, and asking eleven times would be worse than not asking. With one account it states the answer instead of asking (*"Coming out of Checking"*); with none it says nothing and still logs. Every line of the batch - expense, income or put-away - lands on the chosen account.

And after a **photo** is read, the question is raised rather than left to be missed. That is the one path where a whole batch arrives without a single field being touched, so the picker highlights and asks *"which account did these 4 come out of?"* in place, rather than opening a sheet over the rows the person is trying to check.

**Nothing that took work to build dies on one tap** (`recDelId`, `acctDelId`) - from a phone, on the recurring list: *"the delete button is too destructive."* It was. One tap ended a schedule with nothing in between, and a rule is not a row of data - it is the thing standing between somebody and forgetting their rent, so rebuilding it means recalling an amount, a cadence and an anchor date set weeks ago.

The confirm has a job beyond slowing the hand down: it answers the question the hand hesitated over. *"Stop Kristi repeating? No more will post. The 2 already logged stay on Track - this only removes the schedule, never the money that already moved."* People hesitate because they think deleting the rule deletes the paychecks; saying otherwise is the whole point. It is inline on the row, not a dialog, for the same reason everything else here is - a dialog hides the row being asked about.

Accounts get the same treatment plus one consequence more, now that entries name an account: removing one leaves whatever pointed at it with nowhere to be, so the count is stated before rather than discovered after (*"the 2 entries logged against it stay on Track, but stop counting toward any balance until you give them a new home"*).

**The streams you already named** (`knownSources`, `txSourceChoice`, `#txSrcPick`) - from a phone: *"wasn't typing in the source directly contradicting the recurring funds? Why would it be typable if I can just choose from an account I have set up for recurring?"* A straight contradiction, and it was there. The recurring rules on Plan **are** a list of named income streams - Hollywood, Cruise The Creek, Kristi, Bonus paycheck - each set up on purpose with a cadence and an amount behind it. The log form then asked for the name to be typed again from memory, where a typo silently created a second stream matching nothing.

Source is a picker now: rules first (marked *· repeats*, because they were the deliberate ones), then any source used in a past entry, then **＋ Something else** which brings the free text back - a first paycheck has no history to choose from, and a list you cannot escape is worse than one you never had. Choosing a known stream fills the **Type** and the **Account** it was last known to land in, so the rest of the form stops being a quiz about your own history. Anything typed joins the list for next time.

Recurring rules gained an account of their own for the same reason: a rule that does not know where its money lands posts entries counting toward no balance - the same hole the log form had, one step upstream where it repeats every month.

**Where an entry sits in the plan** - the other half of the same message: *"I'm still missing the link between the way Track and Plan are affiliated."* True, and invisible rather than absent. An expense has always carried a category and that category has always had a number on Plan, but nothing on the Track side ever showed it, so the relationship lived only in the data. Opening any expense now shows it: what the category has assigned this month, what is used with this entry included, what is left, and **this entry's share of what has left that category**. Plus the sentence that names the mechanism - *"change its category above and both plans move, the one it leaves and the one it joins."* When the category has nothing assigned, it says that instead and offers the way to fix it, rather than measuring against zero.

**Two budgets, one dollar** (`catWatched`, `dayToDayOn`, `dayToDayHTML`, `state.dayToDay`) - from a phone, on a $225 grocery run the reward calendar called *"$219.26 over"* while the Plan tab said Food still had **$477.35 left**: *"it's part of my planned budget monies that has been accounted for - is this a direct contradiction?"* It was, and it was measurable: two screens, the same $267.65, opposite verdicts - and the calendar disagreeing with its own month-to-date line, which read *"ahead this month."*

The cause is structural. A daily allowance divides a monthly limit by its days and assumes money leaves evenly; groceries arrive in weekly lumps. The calendar was built for **spend mode**, where the Four Walls and zero-based budgeting are switched off and there is no plan to consult - so running both at once had every dollar judged twice by rules that knew nothing about each other.

The allowance is told what it watches. Pick the day-to-day categories - the drip - and everything else is left to the plan, where it was already accounted for. With nothing chosen it watches everything, which stays right for a spend-mode user with no plan to hand off to. A parent covers its subcategories, or the same hole reopens one level down. The money is never hidden: the excluded spend still appears on the day card, named, with *"money your plan already funds, so the allowance leaves it alone - it still counts on Plan, against what you assigned."* In the worked case the day went from *"$219.26 over"* to *"$5.74 saved."*

This is the third instance of the same class, after recurring bills and growth categories: **something the app already knew was committed, being re-judged as though it were a fresh choice.**

**Entries on a calendar day open**, like the identical row on Track always did. Walmart sat there as dead text - two ways of drawing the same thing, one of which quietly did nothing.

**The stale-reference sweep** (audit, section 64) - a deep-dive over everything that points at something else, because the failure is always silent: an id survives the thing it names, and the code reading it produces a wrong number that looks fine. Found and fixed, each healed at three layers (the delete path, load-time `normalizeState`, and for rules the moment of use):

- A `dayToDay` watch list left holding only deleted categories made `catWatched` false for every real one - the allowance watched **nothing**, every day scored perfect, and the streak became fiction. Deleting a category now prunes the list; load prunes ghosts.
- An `acctId` pointing at a deleted account counted toward no balance **and** was skipped by the backfill (which only looks at empty ones) - so the delete-confirm's promise of "a new home" was false. Deleting an account now frees its entries and rules in the same stroke; load clears dead pointers; a rule with one posts through the default.
- The scoped allowance figure leaked into sentences about **total** spending: with only Coffee watched, the spend-mode headline read "you've spent 1% of income" over a month in which $900 of groceries left. The tile, life-hours line and income sentence use total spending now; the limit bar keeps the scoped figure and names both exclusions.
- An edited amount left the **Giving ledger and the Shield** holding the typo - linked records follow the transaction now, same principle as the invest asset.
- Picking a rule-owned income stream never filled the Type, because rules store no `srcType` - past entries of the same stream now fill the blanks a rule leaves.

`sw.js` verified network-first for `app.html`, so no cache bump is needed for updates to reach installed phones.

**Budgeted against actual, at the moment of logging** (`txExpectedFor`, `txAmtNoteHTML`) - drawn on a screenshot in two inks over the Amount field: the figure a stream is **set up** to pay (green, *"budgeted = predetermined"*) and the figure that **actually landed** (red, *"actual"*). The rule already held the first number and the field held the second; the form never put them next to each other, so a short paycheck logged without comment and the shortfall surfaced weeks later as an unexplained reconcile gap, if at all.

Picking a repeating stream now **pre-fills its usual amount** - the usual case costs zero typing. Typing the actual prices the difference live, in both directions: *"$135.22 less than the $2,435.22 this usually is. Worth knowing why before it becomes a pattern."* and *"$164.78 more than the usual. If that repeats, update the rule on Plan so the plan stops understating you."* A hand-typed actual is never clobbered by a stream re-pick, the shortfall is said out loud in the log toast (the question "why was the check short" has a shelf life), and it survives as a fact on the entry sheet after the toast dies: *"Short of usual - $135.22 under the $2,435.22 this stream is set to pay."* A source with no rule behind it invents no expectation.

**An investment lands somewhere** (`destAcctId`, `destAcctOptions`, and the exclusion inside `syncInvestAsset`) - from a phone, on the recurring invest form: *"where it goes should attach to an account."* Right, and the attachment carries a trap the free-text field never had. A tracked destination lives in that account's balance, which already feeds net worth through `bankTotal()`; the auto **Invested capital** asset feeds net worth too. Count a deposit in both and the same dollars print twice; count it in neither - after the destination account is deleted, say - and they vanish. The invariant, enforced at every layer: **counted once, always.**

"Where it went" is a picker now, on the log form, the recurring form and the entry sheet: the tracked accounts (investment kind first), plus *"somewhere I don't track here - just name it"*, which keeps the free text - money also goes into things the app has never been told about, and those keep living in Invested capital exactly as before. A tracked destination makes the transaction a **transfer**: one entry moves the source account's expected balance down and the destination's up. It stays out of the auto asset entirely; deleting the destination account drops its deposits back in, so net worth never loses them; a recurring invest rule carries its destination onto everything it posts, validated at the moment of use like the source account.

**A category can move house** (`moveCatUnder`, `eligibleParents`, `subtreeHeight`) - from a phone, mid-drag, with *Coffee / drinks out* sitting one row above the Food group and no way to tuck it inside: *"coffee and drinks should be able to be reordered and placed in a subcategory under food."* The drag stays **level-locked on purpose** - a thumb slip that quietly re-files a category is worse than a drag that cannot - so the move lives on the category's own sheet under **Where it lives**, which has room to say the part that matters: it is the *same category* afterwards. Same id, so its assignment, its logged history, its repeat rule and its place on the allowance watch list all ride along. Only the filing changes, and a group brings its subcategories with it.

The offered homes are **computed from what is legal** rather than filtered in the UI, so a move the engine would refuse can never appear: never itself, never its own descendants (a category cannot become its own grandchild), and never anywhere that would breach the three-level ceiling once the moving subtree's own height is counted. Top level is always offered as the way back, and the new parent is un-collapsed on arrival so the move is visible rather than filed into a closed drawer.

**"Log an expense" became "Log money moving"** - asked as *"should it read as Log an event?"*. Not *event*, which says nothing about money and is the one thing every line in that panel has in common; but *expense* was genuinely wrong, because the button opens the quick log and that takes money **out, in and put away**. A button narrower than the thing it opens sends people hunting for a second button that does not exist. An older test had pinned the exact phrasing and blocked the correction; it asserts the property now - one button, opening today's log.

**Assign in the rhythm you live in, and price the habit behind the number.** Asked as *"assign this month should hold the frequency of how many times per week by weekly month. They go to get for example a coffee. Somehow that feature got lost - it's hard for people to plan with just one set number if they don't have the options to make a rational decision."* Correct, and the gap was structural: every other money field in the app - the spend limit, the debt budget, the true-rate fields, the recurring hours - carries a period picker through `wireUnitField`. The category assignment, the field people touch more than any other, was the only one locked to a month. It is a unit field now (`csA<catId>`, kinds day/week/biweek/month/year), **remembered per category**, because coffee is a per-week thought and rent is a per-month one and forcing one rhythm on both is what made the field hard to plan with. As everywhere else, the **stored value stays monthly** through `get`/`set` - `$35 a week` is written as `$151.67` and read back as `35` - and the working line spells the multiplication out rather than assuming it.

Under it sits the **habit builder** (`rateBuilderHTML`, `rateMonthly`, `rateOutHTML`): *`3` times `a week` at `$6`* → *3 × $6 a week works out to **$78** a month · 3.3 hrs of your life*, with an **Assign $78** button. Nobody decides "$104 of coffee"; they decide "three a week, about six dollars". Because the builder names the two numbers behind the total, cutting the habit becomes something you can **price** - drop to two a week and watch the month move - rather than a total you shave blind. Its draft lives in `state.rateDraft` (keyed to the category, cleared on load) and the readout patches **in place** on `input`, so the keyboard does not drop mid-number; the button is in the dom from the start, disabled, because a button that only appears once both fields are full means your first tap on a phone lands where a button is about to be. A **pool gets neither control** - its number is whatever its subcategories add up to.

**The ledger line reconciles with its own legend.** Two figures sat inches apart on the net-worth card - the running total at whichever point you tapped, and the all-time total in the legend under the chart - and until you tapped the *last* point they did not match. Reported from a phone with both circled in red: *"these numbers don't agree."* They did agree; the card just never said what the gap was, which is a bad look on the one card whose entire job is telling three near-identical numbers apart. `ledgerRead` now reconciles in both directions: pick a middle month and it names the months since and lands on the figure below (*"Aug since then: down $311.81, which is why the all-time figure below reads $13,388.19"*); pick the last point and it says *"Last point on the line, so this is the same $13,388.19 as the all-time figure below."* The second half matters as much as the first - it teaches the relationship on the tap where the numbers happen to match.

**"Whose money" is a fact about the money, not a preference about the interface.** Asked in the same message: *"this was my spouse's income and therefore it contributes to my balance but not my working hours. What option would I choose when tracking an income?"* None of the eight - every **Type** describes something *you* did (Primary Job, Side Hustle, Freelance, Asset Sale, Skill Monetization, Dividends, Rent, Something else). The answer already existed as `t.owner`, which the arithmetic has always respected (`personalMonthlyIncome()` filters `owner==='a'`, so it powers `effectiveHourly()`), but the picker was `household-only` - hidden behind a checkbox in Settings nobody had found. So the honest answer was unreachable from the screen asking the question, and logging a spouse's paycheque as your own silently inflated your true hourly rate.

The picker is now on every income log and every recurring income rule, for everybody: **Whose money - Mine / Partner / Both of us**, defaulting to Mine and saying nothing until there is something to say. Choosing anything else prints what it changes, where the choice is made: *"Counts in the balance and in every month total, and stays out of **your** true hourly rate and your independence milestones. Kristi earned those hours, not you."* Joint says the rest of it: it stays out of your rate because splitting it in half would be inventing a number neither of you earned. Naming a second earner **turns household mode on by itself** - that is what naming a second earner means - with a toast pointing at Settings for their name and wage whenever they want it. The choice persists between entries on purpose (two paycheques in a row is the household case) and the note stays on screen saying whose the next one will be, because a remembered setting with nothing on screen about it is how you mis-tag your own paycheque.

Independence now follows *whose* it is as well as *what kind*: `isIndieTx(t)` requires `isIndependent(t.srcType)` **and** `owner==='a'`. A partner's freelance cheque is real freelance income and is not your escape from a primary job, so it must not fill your independence bar, trigger the INDIE_WIN voice line, or count as offense in `offenseDefense`. The transaction detail leads with **Whose** when it is not yours, since that is the fact that changes the arithmetic everywhere else on the screen.

**A stream you cannot name is a stream the app cannot reason about.** Reported as *"Source was not accounted for for reoccuring"* - the recurring form on Plan, every field filled but Source, and the Add button live. A recurring **expense** with no category is refused outright; a recurring **income** with no name was quietly stored as `'Income'`. That looks like the same class of default and it is not, because these rules **are** the app's list of named income streams: `knownSources()` builds the log form's Source picker from them, and `txExpectedFor(name)` matches on the **name** to decide whether a paycheck came up short. `knownSources()` dedupes case-insensitively, so two rules both called "Income" collapse into one entry carrying whichever amount loaded last - and a $1,600 paycheck logged against it was told it was **$836.97 short when it was exactly right**. A fabricated shortfall, about the number people are most anxious about, produced by a default trying to be helpful.

Locked at both layers, because rules already saved cannot be un-saved by a form check. **At creation:** a nameless income rule is refused with the cursor placed in the field (*"Give this one a name - Hollywood, Kristi, Bonus - so the app can tell your paychecks apart when you log them"*), an invest rule's destination is equally required, and a duplicate name asks for confirmation naming the consequence. **At the point of use:** `txExpectedFor` returns `0` when more than one rule shares a name, so the app declines to say what either pays rather than confidently reporting the wrong one short. Nothing said beats a confident wrong number.

**And the hours box stops asking a question it throws away.** `recomputeBlendedWage()` filters to `owner === 'a'`, so on a recurring income rule marked Partner or Both of us the "Hours it takes" field was collecting a number nothing would ever read - the same fault as inventing one, pointed the other way. It hides for money that is not yours, and the owner note (now on the recurring form too, not just the log form) says why rather than letting a field vanish unexplained: *"Hours are not asked for it, because only your own hours set your rate."*

**The recurring list is read in the order you think in.** Asked for as *"there should be a reorder tab to organize the reoccurring."* The categories got this months ago, with the reasoning written next to `catOrder`: array order is creation order, and creation order is an accident - the sequence you happened to remember your bills in, not the one you think about them in. The recurring list, which is where the paycheck and the rent live, never got it. **Reorder** now sits above the list exactly as it does above the categories: every row grows a `⠿` grip, drag or arrow keys move it, `recOrder`/`moveRecTo`/`moveRec` write an explicit `sort` (dense, renumbered on every move) so the order survives an import and a restore from backup. The ✕ and ✎ go inert while the mode is on - a destructive button live under a thumb that is trying to drag is a trap.

The decision worth recording is the refusal to build it twice. A second copy of the pointer-drag layer is how two lists quietly stop behaving the same: one gets escape-to-cancel, the other does not; one auto-scrolls at the edge of the glass, the other strands you mid-list. What actually differs between the two lists is four things - where the rows are, what a row is called, how an order is written, what to redraw - so that is what `DRAG_SCOPES` holds, and the grip carries `data-scope`. One engine, two lists, and the structure suite checks the *categories* through it immediately after the recurring list, so the drift shows up if anyone un-shares it.

**Arrangements**, because dragging twelve rows on a phone is a chore: **By what is due next**, **Biggest first**, **Money in, then out**. They appear only in reorder mode, as a starting point rather than a destination - each one *writes* real `sort` values, so what you see is what is stored and the result is still yours to hand-adjust. "Biggest first" prices the **cadence, not the cheque**: $1,600 every two weeks ($3,467/mo) outranks $1,850 a month. Nothing here is a view filter.

**Is Pay Yourself First an expense or an investment?** Asked exactly like that, and the honest answer is **neither on its own** - the app had been quietly answering "expense", which is the wrong half.

It has to be a **plan line**, because zero-based means every dollar gets a job and this one has to compete for the dollar against the fun money. A thing that is not a budget line is "whatever is left at the end of the month" - precisely the failure the Pay Yourself First lesson exists to describe. And the money that moves has to be an **investment**, because an expense is money that is *gone* and this money is *still yours*.

The app already had the machinery: a `growth` tag (`invest` / `save` / `debt`), `catUsed = catSpent + catInvested` so a growth category can be used up by money put away, and `syncInvestAsset` to keep it on the books. The bug was that `runDeepen` created the category with `findOrCreateCat('Pay Yourself First')` and **no growth tag**, so it was neither: the plan counted it, and every dollar somebody paid themselves was filed as spent. It is `'save'` now, the log form refuses to file it as an expense, and `normalizeState` **heals an untagged one on load** - "Pay Yourself First" is a name this app writes and no interface can un-tag a category, so an untagged one can only be a row the app made wrong, and a source fix alone never reaches the people it already happened to. The same pass widened the log form's auto-switch from `growth==='invest'` to `invest` **or** `save`: both are money that moved and is still yours, and only one of them was being caught. (`debt` stays out - a paid-down balance is not a thing you hold afterwards.)

**Category packs.** Asked for as *"these are the things that slowly drive away savings and never get funded until it's too late"* - which is the whole product in one sentence. Nine packs (`CAT_PACKS`): **The essentials**, **Health & wellness**, **Beauty & grooming**, **Online shopping**, **Trips & travel**, **Special occasions**, **Money that works**, **Kids & family**, **Lifestyle**. Every category in them is money people already spend and almost nobody plans for, and the reason is always the same shape: the cost is real, predictable and often annual, but it arrives as a surprise, so it gets paid out of the emergency fund, the fun money or a card. Naming it does not make the money appear - it stops the money being a shock.

The old `Starter set` button dumped eight categories in with no warning and no way to see what they were, which is what prompted *"we got the basic starter set but what's all included?"*. It opens a **browser** now (`#packSheet`): a grid of cards, each with its hook, and tapping one opens the pack's own card - the hook in full, a **truth block** naming what leaving it off costs, then every category with a **reason beside it**, because a list of names teaches nobody anything. *"Christmas is on the same day every year and still arrives as an emergency."* The essentials are in the same list, so what has always been in the starter set is finally visible before it lands.

**Kids & family** is the one that needed a different move. *"A child is not one expense. It is forty small ones, and they never arrive alone."* People do not skip this pack out of laziness - putting a number on your kid feels like putting a price on your kid, so it goes unmeasured, and unmeasured money still gets spent. It comes out of the one line nobody defends: **yours**. The retirement contribution, the dentist, the thing you were going to do at forty. So the card says that out loud before it says anything else, because the guilt is the actual obstacle and a list of category names does not touch it. Ten rows - childcare, school costs, activities, clothes they outgrow rather than wear out, parties and every classmate's gift, babysitting, kid tech, diapers, **family support** (helping a parent or a sibling: real, recurring, almost never on a plan), and an **Education fund** tagged `save`, the only line in the pack that is still yours later.

**Themed textures, drawn in CSS.** Asked for as *"is it possible to add a background or texture to each card themed around the card... I need both a light and dark version."* Every one is a CSS gradient - no image files, because this app is one HTML file that has to work with the network off, and a texture that fails to load is worse than no texture. They scale to any size for free and cost nothing to ship.

**The pattern carries the topic; the colour carries the kind of money.** Stacked courses for the essentials, a pulse going out for health, a fine repeat for beauty, isometric boxes for online shopping, a dashed flight path for travel, wrapping-paper diagonals for occasions, rings spreading from a root for Money that works, scattered dots for Kids & family, a cup-ring repeat for lifestyle. The hue is one of the app's own three: **gold** for the foundations you build on, **terracotta** for the leaks, **sage** for money that keeps working. Nine patterns, three hues, so the set reads as a family and the card tells you something before you have read a word. Both themes come free, because the textures are built from `rgba(var(--pk-rgb), var(--tex-a))` and both `--pk-rgb` and the alphas flip with the theme.

**Two defences on legibility, and it is worth being honest about which one carries the weight.** The `.pk-hook` line is `--muted` at 12px, so it needs 4.5:1; it sits at 5.37:1 on bare `--panel-2` in dark and 4.94:1 in light, and every bit of texture composited under it eats into that. The first draft ran at a stacked alpha of `.205` and put the hook at **3.66:1** - a card that looked better and read worse. The fix that actually carries the load is a **mask** (`radial-gradient` from the top-right corner) that keeps the pattern off the type entirely; the reduced alphas are the margin behind it. Pulling the mask and leaving the alpha high measures 4.16:1, which is how we know which is which.

`layout.mjs` checks this the only way it can be checked: it hides the type, screenshots the surface, and reads the **worst real pixel** behind all 27 text runs on the nine cards, in both themes. The palette suite cannot see this - it reads the stylesheet, and the stylesheet says `--muted` on `--panel-2`, which passes. It also asserts that 27 runs were **sampled**, because a contrast check that silently measured nothing would pass forever. Worst measured: **4.65:1 light, 4.99:1 dark**.

The emoji also gets a tinted chip to sit on (`rgba(var(--pk-rgb),.14)` with a matching border) so it reads as a mark rather than a character that happens to be first, and the card lifts 2px on hover with the pack's own hue on the border.

**Planned / Spent / Remaining.** Asked for as *"a planned / spent / remaining toggle tab may help our plan dashboard be more uniformed with categories"*, and it fixes something real rather than only tidying: **Spent was not on that list at all.** You could see what you assigned and what was left, and had to subtract in your head to find out what had actually gone. The shapes did not match either - a group row printed three figures (rolled-up total, *"$745 direct"*, and available) where a leaf printed two. That is the non-uniformity the ask names.

The list carries **one money column** now, and the toggle says which question it is answering. **Planned** stays the editable one, because zero-based budgeting *is* typing in that column; it becomes a plain figure in the other two, since there is nothing to type into "what you already spent". That is what makes every row the same shape in every mode - the property the structure suite checks by counting children of `.rw-money` rather than by reading prose. Overspending in Spent mode is **marked**, not merely printed. The choice is stored (`state.planView`), because a mode you re-pick on every visit is a button rather than a view.

**A credit card is a balance and a move, and they are different facts.** Asked directly: *"I have a card with a $13,700 limit and a home equity line at $25,000. The question has no clean answer because **two facts are hiding inside one object.** What you **owe** is a balance: it belongs in Accounts, and net worth should subtract it the moment it exists. So both, built as two separate things. A `credit` account kind takes **what is on it** as a positive number and stores it as a **negative balance**, which means every signed sum already in the file comes out right without learning that credit exists. The move is the quietest row in the ledger on purpose - no minus sign, muted colour - because it is the one entry that changed nothing about how much you have.

**The limit is not decoration.** Someone who keeps a $25,000 equity line at zero does not own an empty account; they own **room**, and room is the reason they keep it. **The thing you just logged is the thing you are checking.** Asked as *"why did Fees go 5th in the list if it was the most recent entry?"*

Because the sort only ever compared dates:

```js
list.sort((a,b)=> (a.date<b.date?1:a.date>b.date?-1:0));
```

`Array.sort` is **stable**, so five entries sharing a date kept their position in `state.transactions` - the order they were *typed*, oldest first. Log five things today and the one you just added sits at the bottom of the five, which is the opposite of what a ledger is for: you open it to check the last thing you did.

A transaction carries a date and no time, so there is no clock to sort by. Its **position in the array** is the record of when it was entered, and reading that backwards is the honest answer. Sorted by date descending, then by insertion order descending.

**The reader survives OCR, and deleting an entry is armed.** Two reports off one screenshot: *"the delete button is too destructive"* and *"when adding it was only one thing added and that's the BMV entry."*

The second is the interesting one, and **the first instinct was wrong.** Driving `qlSave` with four filled rows logged four - the save step was innocent. **Reading a bank screen, not just a notepad.** Sent as a screenshot of a bank's pending list read into the quick log: *"it needs to only read the description before Held, the prices are also wrong, a cleared button is also needed."*

The reader was built for a handwritten shopping list, where **one line is one purchase and the number at the end is the price**. A bank screen breaks every one of those assumptions at once, and broke them *silently* - nine rows, all wrong, all plausible enough to log:

| what it read | amount | where the number came from |
|---|---|---|
| `Preauthorization / AI 8...` | $23,952 | the middle of `RR=623952602847` |
| `Aug 27` | $2,026 | a date wearing a price |
| `3913595672 PK...` | $76,271 | another reference |

Two changes, and the first is the one that matters.

**Records are found by the money, not by the line break.** A bank record wraps across as many lines as it likes and ends with an amount, so the text is scanned for real currency tokens (`QL_MONEY`) and everything since the last one is that record's description. A newline means nothing. Requiring the `$` is what makes it safe: reference digits never carry a currency mark.

**The description is cut to the part a person would read** (`qlCleanDesc`): everything from `Held:`/`Exp:`/`Posted:` onward, the `AI=`/`RR=`/`PK=` runs, bare reference digits, stranded timestamps and dates, the screen's own `Pending` heading, a processor tag on the front (`PMT*OH BUREAU MOTOR VEHIC` → `OH BUREAU MOTOR VEHIC`) and a booking code on the end (`AIRBNB * HMREJET25N` → `AIRBNB`).

**The wrapped reference number is the subtle one**, and the reason the first attempt was not enough. A phone splits `RR=623952602847` into `RR=623952` and `602847`, so stripping `KEY=VALUE` leaves an orphan digit run sitting in the merchant name - `Kindle Unltd` came back as `7855866, Kindle Unltd`. The value pattern now absorbs digit-only continuations across the wrap.

Two guards keep it from eating real names. The "cut everything before the last transaction-type lead-in" step only runs when a `KEY=` reference is present - the unmistakable signature of bank plumbing - because without that guard `SQ *PURCHASE COFFEE` loses the purchase and `PAYPAL *PAYMENT` is left with nothing. And the trailing-code strip only fires on something that really looks like a code (letters **and** digits, no spaces), so `Bed * Bath` survives.

With no currency mark anywhere the parser **falls back to the original line-at-a-time reading**, so `coffee 4.50` on a photographed notepad works exactly as it always did.

One thing gained for free: a statement writes every debit as negative, so a minus says nothing - but an **explicit plus** means money arriving, and those rows arrive with the category pre-set to income as a guess you can override.

With nothing typed it just clears; with content it reads `Clear 3 lines?` and needs a second tap, and typing again cancels it. It also drops the photo and the read note, so a bad batch goes away completely without closing the panel.

**The rate layer: it learns your situation instead of reciting it back.** Asked for as *"I care more about it knowing about the interest rate - not so it produces a budget number, but so it learns each person's situation and says hey, you have a high rate on this card, moving it could be beneficial. It should teach instead of regurgitate."*

The blocker was never the arithmetic. A borrowing rate could be typed into **three unrelated places** - a credit account, a liability, the payoff planner - and nothing in the app ever looked at all three at once. So it could know you pay 13% on one thing and 3.6% on another and still have nothing to say about the two facts sitting side by side, which is exactly where the only useful sentence lives.

`pricedLines()` is that one list: normalized, deduplicated by name (the payoff planner imports from liabilities, so the same debt genuinely does appear twice - the copy that knows its rate wins), carrying what is owed, what it costs, whether the line can absorb a balance, and whether anything of yours is backing it. Six signals read it, and they carry `standing:true` because they read your **situation** rather than the month on screen, so they say so and sit behind the month's own reading:

| signal | what it notices |
|---|---|
| `rateSpread` | the dearest balance, priced against the cheapest **revolving** room that could actually take it |
| `rateOrder` | with two balances, what the same dollar is worth against each |
| `rateIdle` | idle cash beside a carried balance, with three months of essentials ring-fenced |
| `rateClear` | cards run through and never carried - the rewards play actually working |
| `rateBlind` | a balance with no rate on it, named as the thing it cannot read |
| `owedTrend` | three readings of what you owe moving the same way |

**Three rules govern every sentence, and they are the difference between teaching and touting.**

1. **It never says do it.** Every signal prices both sides and hands the decision back. The leverage tool already held this line with a regex sweep asserting nothing in it tells anyone to borrow; the rate layer carries its own, with its own control string so a never-fires rule cannot pass by accident.
2. **It names what the cheap rate costs.** A 3.6% line is not cheap because somebody likes you - it is cheap because it is backed by something they can take. That is why credit accounts carry a `secured` flag, asked once at the form: moving a card balance onto a secured line makes the debt cheaper and *changes what happens if a year goes badly*, and a spread quoted without that is advice with the risk edited out.
3. **It says when the answer is "this does not matter".** Below $25 a year the spread signal calls it *"not much... worth knowing the gap exists; not worth an afternoon"* and points at the bigger numbers on the page. Dressing up a $12 finding as a finding is the most dishonest thing a suggestion engine does.

Two smaller honesties fall out of the same discipline. A cheap **mortgage** is never offered as somewhere to put a card balance, because it has no room and cannot take one - only a revolving line with headroom qualifies. And `rateIdle` flips its whole answer when the buffer is thin: *"there is nothing spare to throw at it, and that is the right answer rather than a disappointing one - a cleared card and an empty account is the setup that puts the balance straight back on."*

The snapshot now carries `owed` alongside `bank`, for the same reason `bank` was there: retyping a balance overwrites the old one, so the monthly snapshot is the only place what you owe can ever become a trend. Three readings, not two - two points is a line, three is a habit.

The button counts live and says what pressing it will actually do - `Add 10 categories`, then `Add 7 of 10` - and with nothing ticked it reads **Nothing picked yet** and is disabled, because a button labelled "Add to my budget" that cannot add is a button that lies. `addPack(k, pick)` takes an optional list of names; omitted still means "everything not already on the plan", so the old behaviour is the default rather than a special case. Categories you pass over stay on offer the next time you open the card.

Adding nests the pack under one group (`addPack`), so eight loose rows per pack never turn the plan into a wall of names; the essentials stay flat because they are the top level, not a drawer inside it. `packCatExists` answers to the walls' loose matching for the essentials and to exact names elsewhere, so **adding a pack twice adds nothing** and a card whose contents you already have says so instead of offering itself again. The **Money that works** pack tags what it drops in (`save` / `invest`), so none of it ever reads as spending. And from the second a pack lands, every row is an ordinary category - rename, split, move, target or delete.

**The gate, rewritten by the author.** The welcome card that stands in front of the setup chat was rewritten in his own voice, and the change is not cosmetic. It opens on **"Everybody starts somewhere"** rather than "Everybody starts at zero" - zero is a number, and somebody with savings reads a number as not being addressed. *Somewhere* includes them, and the paragraph under it says so out loud: *"whether there's nothing in your account or plenty."*

The register moved too. The old card said *"I am not real"* and *"I cannot"*; this one is spoken - **"I'm not real"**, **"I can't want this for you"**. Two short lines are lifted out as **beats** (`.iaw-beat`, 19px/900) because at lead size they read as filler and they are the point: *"This is where you are."* and *"What matters is the distance."*

One idea left with the rewrite and it is recorded as a **removal** rather than quietly relaxed into passing: the old card's *"I cannot even waste your time, because I have none to waste"* flipped the burden in a single sentence. The new card makes an adjacent point instead - that easier things exist and cost you time and money too - and the gate list now pins that, plus a thirteenth entry for *"I won't flatter it."* The regexes were rewritten contraction-tolerant, because a check that had pinned a sentence would have failed on a change that lost nothing.

**Destructive actions live where you go on purpose.** The global footer sits under every screen, and `Reset all` sat in it as a plain link one space from `Export data` - a permanent wipe of a browser-only store with no server copy, a mistap away on every tab. The footer now carries only the constructive half (`Install app`, `Export data`, `Import`) plus a **`Start over` signpost** that scrolls Settings' Start-over block into view and flashes it, rather than triggering anything. That block (`#startOver`) states what is lost, offers **Back it up first** (which jumps to and fires the encrypted backup) before **Erase everything**, and `resetEverything()` takes **two** confirmations - the second naming the actual counts ("this erases 3 categories, 2 transactions, 1 goal"), because nobody gets a second chance at this. `Re-run setup chat` moved to Settings for the same reason.

**Photo capture (intake budget photo, quick-log notepad)** - every photo entry point pairs a camera input (`capture="environment"`) with a plain gallery input, behind two separate buttons. A single `accept="image/*"` input lets the browser decide, and in-app browsers routinely offer only the gallery; `capture` forces the camera but hides the gallery, so one input cannot do both jobs. Both inputs share a handler and clear their own `value`, so re-picking the same photo after a bad OCR read still fires `change`.

**Reward calendar (spending mode)** - turns the personal `spendLimit` into a **daily allowance** (limit / days in month) and gives spenders a visual streak to chase (`#rewardCalBox`, `renderRewardCalendar`). A month grid marks each past day: green **✓** under allowance, **★** for a no-spend day, red for over, gold ring on today. Below it: a **This-week pace** strip (spent vs. allowance for the elapsed calendar week, ahead/over), an **under-budget streak** and month **ahead** total, and a month-level nudge (`spendRewardRec`). It's **smart / drill-down**: tapping any past day opens a detail card (`#calDay`, `renderCalDay`, `dayEntries`) showing that day's actual ledger entries (income **+** and expense **-**, by category/source/note), how much was **saved** (allowance minus that day's expenses; income doesn't count against the allowance), and what to do with the difference. If a goal exists, the day offers to **bank** its real surplus toward it (never more than the month's honest surplus). **Units:** a goal holds dollars - its target and progress are dollars - so nobody can bank ten minutes into an emergency fund. In life-hours mode the card keeps **time** for what the day cost you ("1.7 hrs saved", "25 min spent of a 2.1 hrs allowance") and switches to **dollars** for what actually moves ("Bank $40 toward..."), with one sentence joining them: *That's 1.7 hrs of your life you didn't hand over - $40 still sitting in your account.* Goal readouts elsewhere stay in the active unit, since "your emergency fund is 41 hrs of work" is a valid translation; only the **action** has to be dollars. If **no** goal exists yet, the card asks for the one thing that is missing - a name - inline (`.cal-newgoal`), then creates the goal and banks the money in a single press without leaving the screen. It used to jump to the Goals tab and focus a blank four-field form that knew nothing about the day or the amount, so the button promised "bank it toward" and banked nothing. `calSelDay` holds the selection; default is today. Grid `repeat(7, minmax(0,1fr))` keeps the 7th column inside narrow viewports.

**Data-fragility guard (Home)** - because everything is local, a cleared cache or a new phone wipes the data. A dismissible Home banner (`#dataGuard`, `renderDataGuard`) surfaces once the user has real data (`busy>=4` across transactions/assets/goals/liabilities) **and** has never saved a backup (`state.lastBackup` null): "Your data lives only in this browser… back it up." It links to Settings' encrypted backup, hides after a backup is saved, and can be dismissed for the session (`dataGuardOff`). Directly answers the review-panel "data fragility" risk.

*(Removed: in-app affiliate/referral links.* An earlier build surfaced disclosed Acorns/Stash buttons in the reward-calendar day-detail and a matching landing section. Both were **removed** after a StoryBrand review: putting investing nudges next to a user's saved money made the app the very "Trap" it fights, undercutting the trust that is the moat. Money is now made only by **direct support** - see Monetization.md channel #1 / the "pay once to support the build" link.)*

**Household mode (couples)** - opt-in in Settings; one shared budget that understands two earners, no accounts or sync (stays local). Income can be tagged **Earned by** You / Partner / Joint (`owner` on income tx + recurring). Home shows a Household panel: combined income, per-person split with %, and a **proportional fair-share** of the essentials (income-weighted, not 50/50), plus each partner's true wage. The gut-check also prices a purchase in the partner's life-hours. Sharing across two phones uses the encrypted backup (export/restore). Keys: `householdOn`, `nameA`, `nameB`, `wageB`. `incomeByOwner`, `renderHousehold`, `applyHousehold`.

**Funnel (opt-in, all off by default)** - the app stays free and private; money is made *directly*, not by nudging. A "Weekly Gut-Check" newsletter opt-in (Settings) posts only the email to a provider you set, with tone-matched success (`SUBSCRIBE_WIN`/`SUB_ERR`). Two education/support offers reveal only when their URLs are set: a Method Guide and the **"pay once to support the build"** link - the app's honest, primary money model ("pay for privacy"). Config lives at the top of the script: `NEWSLETTER_ENDPOINT`, `GUIDE_URL`, `SUPPORT_URL` (mirror the same values in `index.html`). Framed as **education, not advice**, with a disclaimer. Nothing is gated; financial data never leaves the device.

Transaction `type` is `income | expense | invest | transfer`. Invest reduces the cash
balance but credits the auto-managed `Invested capital` asset (`asset.auto==='invest'`);
deleting the transaction unwinds the asset. Spend streaks, budgets, and the reward
calendar all filter on `type==='expense'`, so investing is never counted as spending.

**Two signed rules, and the difference between them matters.** `txDelta(t)` is the
ledger's rule: income adds, expense and invest subtract, and a **transfer is worth
exactly zero** - moving your own money between your own accounts is not a dollar earned
or a dollar spent. `txAcctDelta(t)` is one account's rule: from that account's point of
view a transfer out is a real subtraction, because the money genuinely left. Every
running-total figure in the app now goes through `txDelta`; `acctLoggedSince` and the
orphan-backfill preview go through `txAcctDelta`. Before transfers existed this
expression was copied six times, and every copy would have made the same mistake in a
different figure.

### Credit cards and lines of credit
`ACCT_KINDS` carries `{k:'credit', liquid:false, owed:true}`. The user types **what is
on it** (a positive number); it is **stored as a negative balance**. That single choice
means every signed sum already in the app comes out right without learning that credit
exists - net worth subtracts it, an expense charged to the card pushes the balance
further negative, a payment landing on it pulls the balance back toward zero.

- `isOwedAcct(a)` / `acctOwed(a)` (owed as a positive) / `acctLimit(a)` /
  `acctHeadroom(a)` (null when no limit was given) / `acctUtil(a)` (percent).
- `assetAcctTotal()` and `owedTotal()` split what `bankTotal()` nets. The identity
  `assetAcctTotal() - owedTotal() === bankTotal()` holds by construction, so the
  assets/owed split on Reflect still adds up to the headline exactly as before - a card
  is simply no longer counted as a negative asset.
- `creditDupes()` catches the one way this double counts: a card tracked as an account
  **and** typed in again under Liabilities. Matched on the normalized name.
- Credit accounts are excluded from `liquidTotal()` (room is not cash) and from
  `destAcctOptions()` (you do not invest into a line of credit).
- `apr` is optional. With two priced lines the accounts summary names which one is
  expensive, in cents per dollar per year.
- `secured` marks a line backed by something you own. It is the one fact that
  decides whether a cheaper rate is worth having, and the rate layer refuses to
  quote a spread without it.

**Recurring rules deliberately have no `transfer` type.** A card payment is usually
"whatever is spare this week", and a fixed monthly rule would be an invented number.
The move is logged by hand.

Backward compatibility: `defaultState()` supplies every key, so older saves upgrade
cleanly (`Object.assign(defaultState(), parsed)`), and `normalizeState()` runs on
every wholesale load (boot, import, restore, sync pull) to clamp invariants (goal
saved ≤ target) and drop records pointing at things that no longer exist.

## Money-psychology layer (Learn, Try-this-instead, Free comfort list)

Behavioral-science grounding applied in the app's own voice (concepts applied, no
source text reproduced). Three surfaces:

**1. Lessons (Learn tab, `LESSONS`) - now 17, split into three arcs.**
- *Mechanics* (original 8): zero-based, pay yourself first, emergency fund, sinking
  funds, snowball vs avalanche, the 24-hour rule, value per dollar, assets vs stuff.
- *Psychology* (new): **Your Money Script** (inherited beliefs; write / trace / test a
  replacement), **Notice, Name, Slow, Redesign, Automate** (the five-step change loop -
  design beats willpower), **Name the Feeling First** (comfort spending answers a feeling,
  not a need), **Look at the Number** (avoidance is negatively reinforced; the five-minute
  exposure fix).
- *Growth* (new - the deficit-to-growth arc): **The Growth Ladder** (see -> buffer -> kill
  interest -> capture the raise -> buy skills -> own things that pay you; one rung, one
  buffer at a time), **Catch the Raise** (lifestyle adapts to income within ~2 months; decide
  the split before it lands), **Cutting Has a Floor** (defense caps at zero, offense has no
  ceiling), **Make Money That Isn't Yours Work** (compounding, honestly framed - the app
  refuses to name investments and points at a licensed professional), **Resilience Is
  Recovering** (a slip is a data point; the review replaces the shame spiral).

**2. Trigger swaps (`TRIGGER_SWAPS`, `triggerSwapHTML`).** The object-keyed `SOLUTIONS`
engine could only answer "want a costly thing -> here's a cheaper thing." Trigger swaps
answer the actual driver, keyed to the trap the user already picked in the gut-check:
*scroll* -> 48-hour list / delete saved cards / unfollow the source; *friction* -> name the
feeling / free comfort list / set a floor not a ban; *status* -> compare to your own last
year / remember what the photo hides / define winning yourself; *leak* -> price the year not
the month / audit renewals / set the cancel reminder now. Both engines can render together.

**Money diary (`state.diary`, Diary tab).** The budget asks for numbers; people
think in stories. An entry is written in plain words under one of four framings
(a win, a hard one, something coming up, just writing), stamped with the date and
time, and kept in a timeline.

The app then READS the entry for money facts and offers to log them - it never
writes anything on its own. `diaryScan` is deterministic pattern matching done
on-device (`diaryAmounts`, `diaryWhen`, `diaryDate`, `diaryCategory`): no model,
no network, nothing to phone home with. A dollar-signed figure with no income
word is treated as money out; a bare number is only read as money when a money
verb sits beside it, so years, house numbers, times and percentages stay out of
the budget. Tense and date come from whichever marker sits CLOSEST to the amount
(`DIARY_REACH`), so a "yesterday" in the first sentence cannot date a plan in the
third. Category is guessed from the words around that amount, preferring a
category the user already has.

Findings become offer cards: past outflow to a category (with a picker),
past inflow as income, and anything future or saved as a goal rather than a
transaction. Approving writes it and records what it did on the entry, so the
timeline shows "logged $340 - Getting Around" under the words that produced it.
Declining leaves the budget untouched. Deleting an entry never removes what it
already logged.

**4. Diary echo (`diaryEcho`, `diaryEchoHTML`, inside the gut-check).** A diary
only read on the day it is written is a notebook. The gut-check surfaces one
entry, chosen by relevance, verbatim, while the cart is still open. Scoring is
keyword overlap with what is being bought (dominant), then entry kind (a hard one
outweighs a note), then recency; a funded plan nudges but does not decide - at a
larger bonus it beat everything for every purchase and the same quote surfaced
every time. Below a threshold it shows nothing: silence beats a non-sequitur.

It is the one surface the register/intensity dial does **not** reach. These are
the user's own words about their own hard weeks, and quoting somebody's grief
back at them in the savage voice is the one place this app must not go. The
framing is identical at clean, blunt and savage, and that is asserted in the
tests.

**3. Free comfort list (`state.comfortMenu`; collected in intake, editable in Settings,
rendered by `comfortHTML`).** The user names, while calm, the no-cost things that actually
help them; the list is surfaced inside every gut-check at the moment of temptation, with
seed suggestions when empty. Comfort spending is an attempt to change a feeling - this
offers a different answer to the same feeling instead of only shaming the purchase.

It is **asked during setup** (`INTAKE` step `comfort`, `renderComfortStep`, just before
`why`) rather than left in Settings. The feature only pays off if the list exists before
the first urge, and every other route to it was post-hoc: wander into Settings, open the
FAB hub, or hit a gut-check and meet the empty state - which asks someone mid-urge to go
do calm reflective setup, the one thing they cannot do right then. The intake step is
chip-taps only and skippable, so it costs about two taps; free-text entry stays on the
Settings card. `commitIntake` unions the picks into any existing list rather than
overwriting, so re-running setup never wipes entries added later.

**Tone constraint carried through:** patterns are framed as things to notice and try, never
as labels or diagnosis (labelling breeds shame and defensiveness, which is what drives
avoidance in the first place). This composes with the existing situation floor - a user in
survival mode still never gets roasted - and the app continues to route serious distress to
a qualified professional rather than treating it in-app.

## Clean: the explanation leaves the screen

Asked for directly: *"all of the content is too long... There should be an option to push a button to learn more information... The content is good. It's just too distracting when the interface should be clean. The extra text should be optional to view. do this for every single topic within the app."*

Measured before touching anything: **4,101 words across ten tabs, 56% of them explanatory prose**, and 5,646px of prose rendered across a 29,801px app - about thirty-three phone screens of scrolling, one in five of them explanation.

A partial answer already existed and was making it worse. **Brief** (`clampPass`) clipped every `.sub` to two lines and put a **More** under it. On a screen with a dozen panels that is two dozen lines of prose to skip past *plus* a dozen extra buttons to ignore: the reading load went down and the visual load went up. Sixty-one blocks on screen, sixty-one controls attached to them.

**Clean** (`sayPass`, `SAY_PROSE`, `sayIsExplanation`, `sayOwnerOf`, `saySheetOpen`) takes the prose out of the layout entirely and gives the **panel** one affordance rather than giving each paragraph its own: a small **?** beside the heading, opening one reusable sheet titled with that panel's name and holding every word that panel would have said. Modes are now **Clean / Brief / Full**, and Clean is the default. Result: **80% less prose on screen** (5,646px to 1,143px) and an app 18% shorter to scroll, with nothing deleted.

### What may be hidden, which is the whole risk

Hide the wrong paragraph and an empty state stops saying it is empty, an error stops naming what failed, or the only instruction on a form disappears. The rule is stated rather than sensed: **an explanation is prose that would read the same if your numbers were different.** Three guards enforce it, in order:

1. **Structural, and nothing overrides it.** Prose inside a form, an error, a status line, an empty state or a live sample (`SAY_LIVE`) is the interface talking about you. It stays.
2. **Authored vs rendered** (`data-say="1"`, stamped on 94 blocks). The runtime cannot tell *"you are $700 away across 1 dream"* - live - from *"$1,000 between you and disaster"* - teaching. Both carry a figure. The **source** can: one is interpolated, one is typed. So the decision is made where the difference is still visible: a prose block that is a static literal with no `${}` anywhere in it cannot be reporting the reader's own numbers. Everything else keeps the conservative rule that any figure means it stays.
3. **Never leave a panel unreadable.** If hiding the prose would leave a panel with no control, no table and no list, the prose *was* the panel. It is left alone.

### Two things the first cut got wrong

**Grouping by the tree instead of by reading order.** `sayOwnerOf` first walked outwards through ancestors looking for a heading, and filed the Four Walls copy under **Do this next** - the first heading an enclosing box happened to own. A reader attributes a paragraph to the last heading they *passed*, so that is what it uses now: scan backwards in document order. Prose that precedes every heading is not a stray - it is the "what is this screen" copy at the top of a tab, and it belongs to the tab, which is what titles it.

**Titling a sheet with the reader's own wage.** `<h2>The job pays $30 an hour</h2>` is a result that happens to be marked up as a heading; using it would have retitled the sheet every time the wage changed. A heading that names a section is short and carries no figure. Accordion heads needed one more turn: they hold their own summary line inside the same element, so `innerText` read as a twelve-word sentence and every one of them was rejected as too long - the name is the first line.

### Keeping it clean after a render

The marks and the buttons live in the DOM, so any render that rebuilds a panel's `innerHTML` throws them away and the prose returns - on the panel the reader just touched, which is the worst possible moment for the screen to fill up again. Rather than patch every render (there are dozens, and the next one written would forget), a debounced `MutationObserver` watches the view that is on screen and re-runs the pass. It ignores its own footprints, so it settles instead of chasing its tail.

One measurement lesson worth keeping: the first pixel counts said 2,581px of prose survived Clean, and most of that was already behind a closed accordion. A closed `<details>` still gets a real box and a real `offsetParent` in Chromium - laid out, simply not painted - so the probe was counting text nobody could see. The same trap `clampPass` had already been bitten by, in the tool built to measure it.

## Home answers two questions

Reported with six screenshots of a single tab: *"All of these are still on one screen of the home, isn't this too intense for a user to look at within an app? That's just one screen, there is entirely too much text and should be replaced with a learn option to explore the unnecessary yet useful information - can it just be hidden until revealed by the user via a separate card?"*

Clean mode had already shortened the paragraphs on Home, and it barely helped, because paragraph length was never the problem. **Home carried fifteen top-level sections.** Three of them answered a question somebody opens a budgeting app to ask. The rest were true, useful, and in the way.

So Home now answers exactly two questions - **where do I stand**, and **what do I do next** - and anything on it has to earn a place under one of them.

**Stays:** the hero, any data warning, the stage bar, **Do this next**, the money cards, the left-to-budget explainer with its two fix buttons, and **Cover First** (which is the next action whenever a wall is unfunded).

**Moves** into one card, *More on your money* (`HOME_MORE`, `homeMoreMount`, `renderHomeMore`, `homeMoreSync`): your true hourly wage, Household, Your evolution, Offense vs. Defense, the "Enough" anchor, why you're here, and your money story. One row each, all shut, one open at a time.

Home goes from **3,105px to 1,831px** on the same data - five screens to two - with nothing removed from the app.

Three details that matter more than they look:

- **The panels are moved, not rebuilt.** They keep their ids, their renderers and their handlers, so no other part of the app has to know this happened. `homeMoreMount` relocates the live nodes once at boot.
- **A panel with nothing to say loses its row.** `renderHomeMore` runs last, after every other renderer, and reads whether each panel actually produced anything this month. An empty panel used to sit on the screen saying nothing; now it does not even get a name in the list. If the open one goes quiet, it closes rather than leaving a highlighted row over nothing.
- **Toggling never rebuilds the row list.** The first cut re-rendered the chips on every tap, which destroys and recreates the button being pressed: a pointer does not care, but a keyboard loses its place on every open, and the row you just activated stops existing underneath you. Opening and closing only toggles classes now.

### Two bugs this shook out

**A recessed surface used as a text colour.** The new rows rendered as washed-out grey that read as *disabled*. The token declaration says why, in a comment written long before this: `--ink` is *"a recessed surface, NOT the text colour: this token is only ever a background"*. It was being used for the row labels and for the body of the explanation sheet. Both are text; both are `--text` now, and a `contrast` probe measures the rendered ratio in both themes (13.05 and 13.71 for the rows, 14.11 and 12.38 for the sheet) so it cannot come back by eye.

**A page-level handle painted over every modal.** The quick-glance drawer's `☰` is `position:fixed` at the left edge, floating over left-aligned text - which is how *"See the ladder"* came to read *"e the ladder"* in a screenshot. It sits on the right now, in the ragged ends of lines. Moving it exposed something worse that had been latent: at `z-index:120` it outranked the modal scrim (60) **and the full-screen intake (70)**, so it had always been drawn on top of them; at the left edge it happened to land on empty space, and on the right it landed squarely on every sheet's close button. It is `z-index:56` now - above the page, below anything modal.

The check that caught it is worth keeping in mind: it asked whether the handle's box overlapped any element's box, and a centred heading fills the width of its box while its glyphs sit in the middle. `Do this next` reported as a collision no eye would ever see. Both the suite and the probe measure **text rectangles** via `Range.getClientRects()` now, which is the thing actually being asked about.

## One collapse pattern, every tab

Reported after the Home cut, with screenshots of Build beside Home: *"Shouldn't the first two screenshots be consistent with the drop-down feature... one shows small pills, all the other emphasizes its own row for the pill. Whatever you choose just make it consistent throughout the entire app."* And: *"all other images show the Shield section, I thought we agreed to make all other areas collapsible pills to condense."*

Both fair. Home had shipped pills; Build still had full-width accordion cards for exactly the same idea; and Shield, Track, Debt, Plan, Diary and Settings had no collapse at all - the Home pass had only ever touched Home.

**The pill wins,** because compactness is the whole point: seven accordion cards are about 980px, the same seven as pills are about 150px. The accordion's one-line summary is the real loss, and it is paid for in the deck's own intro line and the fact that a section name is a name.

`deck` (`deckMount`, `deckPass`, `deckSync`, `deckShow`, `DECK_HEAD`, `DECK_INTRO`) is one component driven from the markup: a panel carries `data-deck="Label"` and that is the whole contract. No table of ids to keep in sync, and a panel added next year is one attribute away from behaving like every other one. On tab activation the deck relocates its panels into a single card, renders one pill each, and opens one at a time.

### The regression collapsing always creates

Hiding a panel breaks every deep link that ends in *"scroll to this field and put the cursor in it"* - Freedom Mode walking you to your hourly rate, the payoff planner walking you to a debt, keyboard reorder moving a row. The target is present and correct and inside a shut section, so the page scrolls to nothing and focus lands on something invisible. Three structure checks caught it immediately.

There are 32 such call sites and the next one written would forget, so the fix is at the two methods every one of them ends up calling:

```js
Element.prototype.scrollIntoView = function(){ deckReveal(this); return si.apply(this,arguments); };
HTMLElement.prototype.focus      = function(){ deckReveal(this); return fo.apply(this,arguments); };
```

`deckReveal` walks up to the enclosing `.dk-panel` and opens it, and is a no-op for everything already visible. Same reasoning that put `applyPanelGates` inside `save()` and stamping inside `stampChanges`: **when a rule has to hold at thirty call sites, it does not belong at thirty call sites.**

### Two smaller things the pass turned up

**A card's padding is not a panel's width.** Nesting a panel inside the deck's padded card made everything in it 32px narrower than it was built for, and at 320px that squeezed the calendar's day cells to 27px - below a thumb. Opened sections break out of the horizontal padding.

**A lone question mark on an otherwise empty tab.** On tabs that are now almost entirely deck, the tab-intro's Clean-mode `?` had no heading to attach to and floated alone at the top like a stray glyph. It hangs off the deck's own heading instead. Build's intro copy also still said *"Net worth is open below; tap any section to expand it"*, which described the accordions it no longer has.

## The dial, put back to work

Reported twice, the second time plainly: *"The three ways of speaking has been lost - blunt and savage vocabulary has been lost, and only applied to the intake."*

Measured before touching anything. Moving Forgiving -> Blunt -> Savage changed **9 of 738 lines on screen. 1.2%.** Plan, Track, Shield, Build, Diary and Learn changed by **nothing at all** - Shield being a whole tab built on confrontation that did not shift a word. The dial was decoration everywhere except the setup chat.

The rule this pass follows, so it does not just become noise:

> **The app's assertions carry the voice. Its labels do not.**

Anything the app says *about you* - your month, your habits, where you stand - moves with the dial. `Target $`, `Add category` and `Save entry` read identically at every setting, because a control that renames itself with the mood is a control you have to re-learn. Savage is a position, not a costume.

Wired: the opening line on Home, the line over the four walls, every tab's opening sentence (Plan, Track, Shield, Debt, Build, Diary), and the **Do this next** steps - which is the most-read block in the app and was seven hard-coded strings.

Result: **21% of the app's 273 assertions now change with the dial**, up from about 3%, and every tab moves. Measured as a share of *assertions* rather than of all lines, because most lines on a screen are labels, figures and category names that must not move.

What stayed flat on purpose, and should: arithmetic explanations (*"Income logged $3,200 - assigned $3,100 = $100"*), mechanical instructions (*"Tap any card to flip"*), and the privacy statements. Tone does not get to edit arithmetic. The existing sensitive-topic floor still overrides everything: a medical bill or the rent is spoken to gently no matter where the dial sits.

## Sharing: there was no door

*"How is a person there to go to settings to sync for the first time - there's no call to action."*

There was not one anywhere. Household mode is turned on in Settings, and the vault is set up four taps deeper inside it - and the collapse pass had just buried it one level further, inside a pill. Two people could run this on two phones for a month, each holding half a budget, with nothing on any screen suggesting the other half existed.

The banner that says *"this budget is not syncing"* already existed for a vault that is **locked**. The state it never covered was a vault that was **never made** - which is the state every household starts in, and the only one that needs an invitation rather than a passphrase. Same banner, one step earlier in the story: **"Sam cannot see any of this"** with a single button, on Home and on Plan, in the voice you chose. It stops asking the moment sharing is set up.

`goSync()` is the one route, so every signpost points at the same door: Settings, the Household section opened, the sync panel rendered *on arrival* and scrolled to. Three details it needs and did not have at first:

- **Mount before opening.** `activateTab` schedules its pass in a frame, so calling `deckShow` straight after was setting a flag on a deck that did not exist yet: the section got marked open and then sat inside a container the pass had left hidden.
- **Render on arrival.** The previous trail into this panel had exactly this bug - it walked you to a box that had not been drawn since the state changed.
- **Not a step in the list.** Home shows three next-steps and a fresh budget fills all three with money basics, correctly. An invitation that only appears once you have nothing else to do is not an invitation.

## StoryBrand: what the audit found

Against the framework - the user is the hero, the app is the guide, one obvious direct call to action per screen, a plan, named stakes:

**Held up.** The user is the hero throughout (*"Every dollar answers to you"*), the app guides rather than performs, Home's hero has a direct CTA with a *transitional* one beside it (**Start budgeting** / **Run a gut check**), and the grunt test passes: what it offers, how life gets better, what to do next.

**Broken by my own collapse pass, now fixed.** Build was a tab with seven pills and **not one button** - a screen that asks you to browse is a screen nobody acts on. The deck carries a direct CTA now (`DECK_CTA`) for the tabs whose whole content sits inside it, and Learn's *"Try this"* action - the one thing to do on that tab - was styled as an aside rather than as the action.

**One measurement mistake worth recording.** The audit first reported Reflect as having no call to action at all, and I nearly gave it one. It has two: this app has more than one name for *"this is the action"* - `.primary` on a button, `.solid` on a trail button - and the audit was only counting the first. **A tool that measures one spelling of a thing will report the other spellings as missing.**

Still open and not invented away: Settings has no call to action, which is correct for a settings screen, and Reflect's own CTAs only render once the month has enough logged to report on. Neither is a regression.

## The running-balance column

Reported with the bank's own screen beside the result: a page of Acorns withdrawals came back with every real amount **plus a row for every running balance** - `N 855-739-2859 / $5,311.75` sitting next to the transaction it was the balance after. Half the rows were not transactions at all.

Every line on a bank statement carries two numbers, and the reader was taking both.

Guessing by size would be wrong - a $5,311.75 transaction is perfectly possible, and an app that silently drops your largest amounts is worse than one that adds a few. But a balance column can be **proved**, because it is arithmetic: listed newest first, each balance is the next one minus the amount between them. From the report that started this:

```
5,334.25 − 22.50 = 5,311.75   ✓
5,380.05 − 45.80 = 5,334.25   ✓
5,111.75 − 200.00 = 4,911.75  … and so on down the page
```

`qlDropBalances` splits the extracted stream into its two interleavings and tests that relation on each. A column is only dropped when most of the pairs agree - two agreeing pairs is a coincidence, most of them agreeing is a column - and the reader then says how many balances it set aside, because a reader that quietly halves your statement is indistinguishable from one that missed half of it. A statement with no balance column is untouched, which the probe checks explicitly.

**Also fixed in the same report.** `== 2° Nf 2°. G&G) <|Se` came back as a transaction *name*. A description that is mostly not letters is OCR soup, and showing it is worse than showing the row as unnamed: a person will fix a blank, and will not think to question something that looks like it was read properly. The amount is kept either way - that is the part OCR gets right and the part that is tedious to retype.

**And the other half of the report:** *"it didn't let me upload multiple pictures."* The dedicated statement reader took a batch; the Quick Log's own picker took one file, and its handler read `files[0]`. Both are fixed - the picker accepts a batch, the camera *adds* a shot rather than replacing (which is how you photograph page two), pages are thumbnailed in reading order, and the read pass OCRs each page and parses the joined text **once**, so a transaction split across a page break is still one statement.

## A put-away belongs somewhere

Asked with three screenshots and exactly the right question: *"So all of these did not land in investing for acorns. Should I delete the investing category? And if it didn't land there where did it go? I thought I put it here if it doesn't belong. Is it a duplicate?"*

No, nothing was duplicated, and the Investing category should stay. The money went **nowhere visible**, and that was my fault, from a shortcut I left a comment admitting at the time:

> *"Two entries at the bottom of the same dropdown, so nothing new has to fit on a phone row."*

`→ Put away (savings or investing)` sat in the **same list as the categories**, so it was picked *instead of* one. Those are two different questions - **which pool does this belong to**, and **did the money actually leave you** - and one dropdown can only answer one of them. The entry came out as `type:'invest'` with no `catId`: it counted toward net worth, it appeared on Track with its arrow, and it never touched the Plan line. Acorns read `$0` while $462.20 had plainly gone somewhere.

Worse, it could not be repaired: the transaction edit sheet only offered **Category** when `type==='expense'`, so an invest entry had no way to be filed even one at a time.

Three fixes, and the counting needed none of them - `investedFor(catId, month)` was already correct and had simply never been given a `catId`.

**The category decides the verb.** `growthOf()` walks up the tree, so "Acorns" under "Investing" is an investing category without having to be told twice, and choosing it records a put-away **in that category**. One choice, both facts. `→ Put away` stays as the fallback for money that genuinely has no category.

**An invest row can be filed.** Category now appears on the edit sheet for invest as well as expense.

**And the ones already logged are offered back.** `strandedPutAways` finds invest entries with no category, and Plan shows how many there are and what they total, with a picker of only the categories money can be put away into and one button that files all of them. It disappears when there is nothing left to file. Nobody should have to fix twelve entries one at a time to recover from a dropdown that asked the wrong question.

The general lesson is about the shortcut, not the bug. **Two orthogonal questions crammed into one control will be answered as if they were one question** - and the person answering will be right, by the interface's own logic, while the data comes out wrong.

## Four from one screenshot batch

### Carrying in only the accounts you mean

*"How could I plan a whole month from income that isn't logged but it won't let me add the bank balance from the previous month? Left to budget will always be negative. I don't want to add the full 83706 but I should be able to carry certain accounts in my savings."*

The carry button committed `bankTotal()` - every account at once. For anybody whose savings and retirement live in the same app as their chequing, that is not an offer, it is a dare, and the only usable answer was to decline it. So Left to budget stayed negative all month for a plan that was actually funded.

`carryPickHTML` asks instead of guessing, because which accounts fund *this* month is a judgement nobody else can make. What it does bring is a sensible start: **what you spend from is ticked, what you have put away is not**, because carrying a retirement balance into a grocery budget is how a plan starts lying to you. A credit card is never offered at all - that is money you owe. Money already logged as arriving this month is subtracted, so nothing counts twice, and the running total moves as you tick, before anything is committed. The choice is stored as `openingFrom[month]` - **which accounts**, not just the number it produced.

**Playwright found a second bug while I was screenshotting the first.** A strict-mode locator refused to click `[data-carryin]` because it matched *two* elements: the left-to-budget note is drawn on Home **and** on Plan, so every control inside it exists twice. The handler reached for `document.querySelector('[data-carrywrap]')` and got Home's copy - so tapping the button on Plan opened the picker on a screen nobody was looking at. It opens the one belonging to the button that was pressed now. A test refusing to act on an ambiguous selector is a better bug report than most bug reports.

### The calendar moves while the app is open

*"Now is September... Not August."* Your evolution said Start Aug, Now Aug, and listed one month, on the 4th of September.

`captureSnapshot()` runs in `boot()`, and **`boot()` runs once - at page load**. An installed app resumed from the background for days never boots again, so the month turns over and nothing notices: no snapshot for the new month, and a history that stops at whenever the app was last cold-started. The same lesson the tripwire code on this page had already learned, in a comment sitting forty lines away: *"the app is usually still open in the background... boot() never runs again."*

`monthWatch()` checks on `visibilitychange`, on `focus`, and hourly for a phone left on the table over midnight on the 1st. Coming back to the app is the moment to check, and it is free.

### A pill that opened nothing

*"What you've told me doesn't do anything. It's a dead action."*

Two panels carried an inline `style="display:none"` and set it themselves when empty. **An inline style outranks a class rule**, so the deck toggled `dk-on` and the panel did not move. Both use the `hidden` attribute now - which is the one `deckLive` already reads, so an empty panel is not offered a pill at all rather than being offered a dead one. Display inside a deck belongs to the deck.

The general shape: **when two mechanisms can both set the same property, the one that wins is decided by CSS specificity rather than by intent** - and the loser fails silently, which is how a control comes to do nothing at all.

## Showing the work, so it can be acted on

Two faults circled on one panel: *"The purple should be actionable and blue is still not formatted enough to actually know what it is."*

**Purple** was `and 16 more, all of them on Track` - a sentence that *names a place instead of opening one*. The thing somebody wants at that moment is the other sixteen, here, where they are already looking. It is a control now: **Show the other 16 ↓**, which unrolls the rest in place and offers to fold them back. Open state is per account, so opening one card does not unroll every other one on the screen.

**Blue** was `© + = £1 = Accounts Move Money Check` printed as though it were a name somebody could recognise. It is an OCR read that never happened, logged before the statement reader learned to reject that. Rendering it verbatim asks the reader to identify something unidentifiable - so the row says **Needs a name** instead, and **every row is now a button that opens the entry it stands for**, because this list is exactly where a wrong figure gets noticed, and noticing it while being unable to touch it was the whole complaint.

The readability test needed a second attempt, and the first one is the interesting part. The rule from the statement reader - *a name that is mostly not letters is soup* - passed this string, because `© + = £1 = Accounts Move Money Check` is **63% letters**. The damage is all in the prefix. What actually gives it away is characters a bank descriptor never contains: a copyright sign, a currency symbol that is not the one you bank in, an equals sign. Anything outside the set a real statement line uses means the read went wrong, however much of the rest survived. The test is also scoped to free text only - a transfer label and a category name are composed by this app, and questioning those would be the app questioning itself.

**And the working was being cut off.** `$6,637.64 + $1,230.23 − $1,881.04 − $507.20 = $5,4` ran off a 390px screen mid-figure. That is the one number in this app that may never truncate, because the panel exists to show its arithmetic - a total you cannot read is worse than no total, since it looks like an answer. It wraps now, and a check asserts the equation ends on a complete figure rather than merely that the panel fits.
